The Marvel Cinematic Universe is the most commercially successful film franchise in history. Through 42 titles in the VirtueVigil database, spanning from 2002's Spider-Man (included because his 2021 MCU integration makes the comparison essential) through 2026's Daredevil: Born Again Season 2 and Avengers: Doomsday, the MCU has generated more revenue, more cultural conversation, and more arguments about ideological content in popular entertainment than any other single franchise.

The question this list answers is specific: across 42 films, TV seasons, and special presentations, what is the ideological character of the MCU? Which entries push progressive agendas hardest? Which ones stay grounded in the traditional values of heroism, sacrifice, and duty that made the superhero genre work in the first place? And what does the pattern across all 42 titles tell us about where Marvel Studios is headed?

Every ranking on this page is determined by the VirtueVigil Woke Scoring System (VVWS), a dual-metric methodology that scores woke content and traditional values content independently, then calculates the margin between them. The Woke Score measures progressive ideological content across severity, authenticity, and centrality dimensions. The Traditional Score measures traditional values: courage, sacrifice, duty, family, honor, faith. A negative margin means the progressive content outweighs the traditional. A positive margin means traditional values carry the film. Rankings run from most woke at the top to most traditional at the bottom. Each entry links to the full VirtueVigil review with complete category breakdowns, trope audits, and parental guidance assessments.

The distribution tells a story: one title in WOKE territory, four in BALANCED WOKE, one in WOKE LEAN, and thirty-six in BALANCED or traditional-leaning territory. The MCU's center of gravity is unmistakably traditional, but its outliers get the headlines. Here is the complete picture, ranked most woke to most traditional.

The Big Picture: What 42 MCU Titles Tell Us About Marvel's Ideological Arc

The MCU's ideological distribution across 42 titles tells a clear story:

One WOKE title (Daredevil: Born Again Season 2 at -11.6). One WOKE LEAN title (Captain Marvel at -16.2, though its raw margin is worse than the verdict suggests). Three BALANCED WOKE titles (The Marvels, Eternals, Black Widow). Together, these five films represent the MCU's progressive vanguard -- and every single one of them was released after 2018.

Twelve BALANCED titles (from Thor: Love and Thunder at -4.1 to Captain America: Brave New World at +3.8). These films sit in the narrow band where traditional and progressive content roughly cancel out, and they represent the MCU's default mode in Phase Four and Five: enough traditional heroism to keep audiences engaged, enough progressive elements to satisfy the studio's institutional commitments.

Fourteen BALANCED TRADITIONAL titles (from Venom at +6.1 to Avengers: Age of Ultron at +14.5). This is the MCU's comfort zone -- films where the traditional hero's journey carries the day, with progressive elements present but not dominant. The Avengers films cluster here, as do the Spider-Man films, the early Thor entries, and the Guardians trilogy.

Six TRADITIONAL LEAN titles (from Captain America: The Winter Soldier at +14.6 to Ant-Man at +17.1). These are films where traditional values drive the narrative with only minor progressive counterweights.

Four TRADITIONAL titles (Daredevil: Born Again at +17.2, Deadpool and Wolverine at +19.7, The Fantastic Four: First Steps at +21.1, Captain America: The First Avenger at +23.1, and Spider-Man at +23.2). These are the MCU's most traditional films, and the list tells its own story: two are from Phase One, one is a Fox acquisition, one is a TV series, and one is a 2025 release that reads as a deliberate corrective to Phase Four's excesses.

The pattern is clear. The MCU's ideological center of gravity shifted left during Phase Four (2021-2023), producing its most woke titles (Eternals, The Marvels, Black Widow) and several BALANCED films with progressive lean. Phase Five (2024-2026) shows signs of rebalancing: Deadpool and Wolverine (+19.7), Daredevil: Born Again (+17.2), The Fantastic Four: First Steps (+21.1), Spider-Man: Web of Heroes (+17.6), and Avengers: Doomsday (+13.8) all sit firmly on the traditional side of the ledger. The Phase Four era of ideological experimentation appears to be giving way to something more traditional -- not because Marvel Studios had a change of heart, but because the market delivered an unambiguous verdict. The Marvels lost money. Eternals divided audiences. Captain America: Brave New World underperformed. The films that worked (Spider-Man: No Way Home, Deadpool and Wolverine, Guardians Vol. 3) were the ones built on traditional foundations.

The exception that proves the rule is Daredevil: Born Again Season 2, which arrived in 2026 with the highest Woke Score in the MCU database at 25.9. It sits at the top of this list as a reminder that the ideological machinery inside Marvel Studios is still running. The question is whether the market will continue to fund what audiences keep rejecting.
