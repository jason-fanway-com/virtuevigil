Hollywood spent the first half of the 2020s telling audiences what to think. The industry's most celebrated films of the decade, Anora, Emilia Perez, Conclave, Barbie, are ideological arguments first and entertainment second. The headlines, the awards, the festival circuit buzz all flowed toward the films that affirmed progressive orthodoxies. But here is what the headlines missed: the films audiences actually remembered, the ones they watched twice, the ones they showed their kids, were almost never the ones winning Palme d'Or at Cannes. They were the films that told a story worth telling without a lecture attached.

VirtueVigil has now reviewed over 760 films and shows. Across that database, spanning 2020 through 2026, a quiet counter-current is visible. While the industry's prestige apparatus rewarded ideological conformity, a separate set of films quietly built the decade's actual traditional canon. Some of them, like Sound of Freedom and Reagan, were openly dismissed by critics precisely because their values ran against the grain. Others, like Ne Zha 2 and The SpongeBob Movie, were so commercially dominant that nobody could ignore them. And a few, like The Death of Robin Hood from A24 of all places, proved that even the most progressive studio in Hollywood will fund a deeply Christian story when the craft is undeniable.

This list ranks the 20 most traditional films of the 2020s so far, determined by VirtueVigil's VVWS dual-metric scoring system. The Traditional Score measures traditional values present, the Woke Score measures progressive ideological content, and the Margin is the gap between them. A positive margin means the film's traditional content outweighs its progressive content. Every entry links to the full VirtueVigil review with complete scoring methodology, trope breakdowns, and parental guidance. These are not the films the industry celebrated. They are the films the audience kept. Ranked from most traditional to least.

#1 (Most Traditional): Reagan (2024)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 44.3 WOKE: 2.4 MARGIN: +42 TRAD

Genre: Biography / Drama / History • Year: 2024 • Platform: Theatrical

Reagan is not a biopic. It is a monument. Sean McNamara's film, 14 years in the making, does not attempt to understand Ronald Reagan. It does not wrestle with his contradictions, interrogate his failures, or explore the human complexity behind the myth. It worships him. Every frame, every musical cue, every narrative choice is designed to reinforce a single thesis: Ronald Reagan was a great man, chosen by God, who saved the world from communism. The +42 margin is the largest traditional margin in our database for a live-action film. A woke score of 2.4 means there is almost no progressive content to speak of. Whatever you think of Reagan the man, the film is what a traditional values picture looks like when it refuses to apologize.

#10: Sound of Hope: The Story of Possum Trot (2024)

The story of Possum Trot, Texas, is one of the most extraordinary stories in American religious life in the last thirty years. A small church in a town most people could not find on a map. Over twenty years, 22 families in that church adopted 77 children from the state foster system, most of them children with trauma histories so severe that the professional system had given up on placing them. Sound of Hope tells this story through Donna Martin, the woman who heard a call and answered by building an entire community around the idea that every child deserves a family. The woke score of 2.0 is negligible. The traditional score of 29.8 comes from a film about faith in action, about families doing the hardest work there is, about the proposition that love is not a feeling but a commitment that costs everything.

#12: A Great Awakening (2026)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 28.7 WOKE: 0.3 MARGIN: +28 TRAD

Genre: History / Drama / Faith • Year: 2026 • Platform: Theatrical • Director: Joshua Enck • Cast: John Paul Sneed, Jonathan Blair

The Whitefield-Franklin friendship is one of history's most interesting odd couples, and it deserves to be seen. George Whitefield was the greatest preacher of the First Great Awakening, a man whose voice could reach 30,000 people in an open field. Benjamin Franklin was a deist, a skeptic, a man of science who did not share Whitefield's faith but admired him enough to publish his sermons and become his friend and printer. A Great Awakening tells their story with care, craft, and a complete absence of ideological interference. The woke score of 0.3 is as close to zero as anything outside pure faith cinema gets. Take your family. Take your small group. Take the skeptic in your life who thinks faith stories are all saccharine nonsense.

Mercy is a strange entry on this list because it has the highest traditional score of any film here, 44.0, but also a woke score of 15.8 that reflects its heavy-handed AI ethics messaging. #20: I Swear (2026)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 24.8 WOKE: 0.3 MARGIN: +25 TRAD

Genre: Biography / Drama • Year: 2026 • Platform: Theatrical • Director: Kirk Jones • Cast: Robert Aramayo, Maxine Peake

I Swear is one of the most profoundly human and profoundly traditional films in recent cinema. It celebrates family love, personal courage, and the dignity of every human being. It rejects shame, cruelty, and the reduction of persons to their conditions. It shows that the measure of a person is not their appearance or their difference, but their heart. Robert Aramayo plays John Davidson, a man with Tourette syndrome who fought for the right to live a normal life, to work, to love, to be seen as a person rather than a diagnosis. A woke score of 0.3 reflects a film that has absolutely no interest in progressive ideology. The traditional score of 24.8 comes from a story about the irreducible dignity of the individual, treated not as a political argument but as a self-evident truth. It closes this list for a reason: because the most traditional value of all is the belief that every life has meaning, and films that actually believe that are rarer than they should be.
