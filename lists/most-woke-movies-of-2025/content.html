2025 was the year Hollywood stopped pretending. The ideological content that previous years buried in subtext, allegory, and careful studio notes went fully explicit in 2025. Major releases from Disney, A24, Netflix, and Warner Bros. carried political frameworks so openly that nobody on either side of the culture war had to guess what a film believed. The films declared themselves.

VirtueVigil scored every 2025 release using our dual WOKE/TRAD methodology. This list ranks the 20 most woke films of the year by net margin: the gap between the weight of progressive messaging and the resistance offered by traditional values, authentic characters, and earned storytelling. Rankings run from highest woke margin to lowest. A negative margin means woke content dominates. The further negative, the stronger the woke signal.

Every entry links to the full VirtueVigil review with complete trope audit, parental guidance, and scoring breakdown. The methodology is transparent, the scores are deterministic, and the entries are ranked by math, not mood.

#1: Zootopia 2 (2025)

STRONGLY WOKE WOKE: 91.4 TRAD: 53.0 MARGIN: -38.4 WOKE

Studio: Walt Disney Animation Studios • Year: 2025

Disney's most aggressive ideological delivery system returned in 2025 and turned the dial past anything the original attempted. Zootopia 2 takes the franchise's prejudice allegory and escalates it into a full systemic-exclusion narrative: reptiles were exiled from Zootopia decades ago by a wealthy founding family that stole a reptile inventor's work, framed her for murder, buried her district under Tundratown, and built a fortune on the lie. The parallels to colonialism, stolen land, erased contributions, and manufactured fear of the other are not subtext. They are the load-bearing structure. The WokeScore of 91.4 reflects institutional evil, historical revisionism, colonialist villain framing, therapy culture normalization, and coded LGBTQ+ representation all weighted by severity and centrality. The TradScore of 53.0 is substantial and reflects something actually interesting: Judy Hopps and Nick Wilde are cops, and they are heroes. Not ambiguous. Not compromised. The film trusts law enforcement, evidence gathering, and legal process as the mechanisms of justice. Reform within the system, not destruction of it. That is a classically conservative position embedded inside a film that is otherwise the most woke animated release of the year. The tension is real. The conversation with your kids afterward is worth having.

#2: Bugonia

STRONGLY WOKE WOKE: 28.9 TRAD: 8.5 MARGIN: -20.5 WOKE

Director: Yorgos Lanthimos • Stars: Emma Stone, Jesse Plemons, Aidan Delbis • Year: 2025

Yorgos Lanthimos's black comedy thriller is a skillfully made, darkly funny, and ultimately nihilistic anti-capitalist manifesto disguised as an alien conspiracy kidnapping story. Two conspiracy-obsessed men kidnap a pharmaceutical CEO (Emma Stone, shaving her head for the role) believing she is an alien overlord killing Earth's honeybees. The twist: she actually is an alien, and after surveying humanity's record of violence, environmental destruction, and corporate greed, she calmly annihilates every human being on Earth. The final image is bees returning to their hives in a world scrubbed clean of people. The message is not subtle: humanity is a failed experiment, and the planet would be better off without us. Stone and Plemons are extraordinary in their roles. The craft is undeniable. The ideology is explicit and final: human extinction as ecological salvation. This is one of the most ideologically loaded films of any year.

#3: Clown in a Cornfield

WOKE WOKE: 23.0 TRAD: 5.0 MARGIN: -18.0 WOKE

Director: Eli Craig • Year: 2025

A competently made slasher with a political agenda it conceals until the midpoint and then delivers with zero ambiguity. A teenager moves to a failing Missouri town where someone dressed as the local mascot starts killing teenagers. The killer is revealed as the town's establishment: the mayor, sheriff, teacher, and shopkeeper, organized in a coordinated murder campaign. The mayor also burned down the town's economic foundation himself and blamed the young. The allegory could not be more direct: conservative small-town America blames outsiders for decline that was actually caused from within by the people claiming to defend tradition. The source novel was written during the first Trump term with this allegory explicit and intended. The craft is solid. The politics are structural.

#4: Eddington

WOKE WOKE: 23.7 TRAD: 8.2 MARGIN: -15.5 WOKE

Director: Ari Aster • Stars: Joaquin Phoenix, Emma Stone, Pedro Pascal, Austin Butler • Year: 2025

Ari Aster's fourth film is the one he has been building toward since Hereditary. Each previous film destroyed one institution: the family (Hereditary), the romantic relationship (Midsommar), the male self (Beau Is Afraid). Eddington destroys the conservative community. Joaquin Phoenix plays a small-town New Mexico sheriff navigating the COVID pandemic, a likable local lawman who descends into conspiratorial extremism. The film argues this descent is not individual failure but cultural infrastructure: conservative distrust of expertise, suspicion of institutions, and prioritization of individual interpretation over collective guidance create the ideal medium for extremist growth. The progressive argument is presented as fact. The conservative argument is presented as symptom. Phoenix is extraordinary in the role. Aster is brilliant. The film is technically masterful and substantively wrong. Both things are true.

#5: The Wedding Banquet

WOKE WOKE: 19.0 TRAD: 4.0 MARGIN: -15.0 WOKE

Stars: Bowen Yang, Lily Gladstone, Joan Chen • Year: 2025

The 1993 original was a genuinely moving film about a gay man caught between the love he had chosen and family expectations he could not refuse. The 2025 remake makes a different choice: it starts from a fully visible queer household, complete with a PFLAG-active mother, and rebuilds the farce around a visa problem rather than closeted identity. Bowen Yang and Lily Gladstone are charming. Joan Chen is the film's best character. But the remake's expansion to four queer characters, two relationships, a visa problem, an IVF subplot, and a disapproving Korean grandfather dilutes the original's emotional precision. The film's thesis is clear: tradition is fine as long as it eventually affirms queer identity. That is a progressive argument wearing a traditional-family-comedy costume. The original is better and is on streaming.

#6: Now You See Me: Now You Don't

WOKE WOKE: 18.0 TRAD: 7.0 MARGIN: -11.0 WOKE

Year: 2025

A glossy, crowd-pleasing heist fantasy that repeatedly moralizes theft as justice and uses modern political talking points as applause lines. The film's core feel-good engine is not merely clever magic; it is redistribution, anti-elite resentment, and climate scolding dressed up as spectacle. Robin Hood theft is treated as virtuous rather than corrupting. Climate messaging arrives as sermon rather than backdrop, delivered directly at rich villains with Mother Earth language and private-jet guilt. Corporate evil is painted as a monolith. The film does not fully abandon right and wrong; it simply insists that right means vigilante redistribution. On the traditional side, there is genuine loyalty, sacrifice, and moral clarity woven through the story, which makes the ideological packaging more effective, not less.

#7: Presence

WOKE WOKE: 18.1 TRAD: 8.5 MARGIN: -9.6 WOKE

Director: Steven Soderbergh • Stars: Lucy Liu, Chris Sullivan, Callina Liang • Year: 2025

The most technically inventive horror film of early 2025 is also a quietly progressive film wearing an arthouse horror mask. Shot entirely from the ghost's point of view, the camera drifts through a suburban home watching a family fracture. Ambitious mother Rebekka (Lucy Liu) is engaged in white-collar crime and emotionally distant. Son Tyler is a bully and catfisher. Father Chris is warm but powerless. Daughter Chloe is sensitive, grieving, and the only family member the presence protects. Soderbergh and screenwriter David Koepp are not subtle about where the film's sympathies lie. The ghost sides with Chloe because Chloe sees the family clearly. The traditional family as protective institution is the film's villain, at least in the form it takes in the Payne household. The craft is undeniable. The ghost has a worldview, and that worldview is progressive.

#8: The White Lotus: Season 3

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 14.5 TRAD: 5.5 MARGIN: -9.0 WOKE

Creator: Mike White • Stars: Walton Goggins, Jason Isaacs, Parker Posey, Sarah Catherine Hook • Year: 2025

Mike White's third season takes the luxury-resort mortality play to Thailand and delivers the most sprawling chapter yet. Traditional family structures hide corruption. Masculine authority is fraud. Wealth corrodes. Walton Goggins is the season's most compelling presence as a working-class interloper whose unpredictability carries genuine threat. Jason Isaacs plays a man performing paternal authority while a criminal catastrophe closes around him. The three-women-on-vacation subplot (Carrie Coon, Michelle Monaghan, Leslie Bibb) is the season's weakest element: accomplished actors given thin material about middle-aged disappointment. The show adds Western appropriation of Eastern spirituality to its satirical targets, and one character's Buddhist seeking is treated with sympathy that no Christian equivalent would receive in the same show. Consistently progressive in its satirical targets and consistently excellent in its craft. Conservative viewers who have enjoyed the show despite its politics can continue to do so with eyes open.

#9: Mother Mary

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 28.4 TRAD: 19.8 MARGIN: -8.6 WOKE

Director: David Lowery • Stars: Anne Hathaway, Michaela Coel, Hunter Schafer • Year: 2025

David Lowery's first major engagement with the machinery of celebrity and pop music is a film about female power, intimacy, and the cost of being watched. Anne Hathaway plays Mother Mary, an iconic pop star whose career was built on her estranged best friend Sam's (Michaela Coel) designs, designs for which Sam was never credited, acknowledged, or compensated. The film's framing of power argues that Mother Mary's silencing of Sam was not simply individual betrayal but the predictable outcome of a celebrity machine that demands women compete for limited space. The world is almost exclusively female and queer-coded. The emotional architecture centers female intimacy, anger, desire, and creative power. Lowery's extraordinary craft keeps it from feeling like a lecture, but the progressive framework is structural. Original songs by Jack Antonoff, Charli XCX, and FKA Twigs function as emotional punctuation rather than traditional numbers.

#10: Avatar: Fire and Ash

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 21.6 TRAD: 13.4 MARGIN: -8.2 WOKE

Director: James Cameron • Year: 2025

James Cameron has now made three Avatar films. Each one is technically extraordinary and ideologically predictable. Fire and Ash is the best-looking film of 2025 and an almost proud repetition of every idea the franchise put on the table in 2009: anti-military messaging, noble-indigenous-versus-corporate-colonizers framework, the white protagonist who becomes the best version of the culture he joined. The RDA is always corporate militarism. The Na'vi are always the indigenous people whose land is being stolen. The human military characters exist to be wrong about everything. Cameron has been making this film since Aliens, and he does not apologize for it. But the family themes are genuinely strong, the grief is handled with restraint, and the Ash People introduce moral complexity the franchise sorely needed: not all indigenous people are peaceful, not all enemies of your enemy are your friends. The spectacle is staggering. Three films of the same sermon may be enough.

#11: Adolescence

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 22.5 TRAD: 14.8 MARGIN: -7.8 WOKE

Creator: Jack Thorne • Stars: Stephen Graham, Owen Cooper, Erin Doherty, Ashley Walters • Year: 2025

The most important piece of television produced in 2025, and that is not the same thing as saying it is the best. Armed police raid a family home at dawn and drag 13-year-old Jamie Miller out of bed on suspicion of murdering a classmate. Over four episodes, each shot in a single unbroken take, the series examines the arrest, the school's response, Jamie's psychological evaluation, and the family's collapse. The one-take format is the show's thesis expressed through form: you cannot cut away. Owen Cooper, cast at 13 with zero professional experience, plays Jamie as neither monster nor innocent. His performance is devastating. Erin Doherty as the forensic psychologist earned an Emmy for peeling back Jamie's defenses. The show's thesis is that the internet, specifically the manosphere and incel communities, radicalized a normal boy into a killer. It does not seriously explore alternative explanations: the decline of father-son relationships, the absence of faith or community, or the failure of schools to provide meaning. Nine Emmy wins. 146 million views. Extraordinary craft. Progressive framework.

#12: KPop Demon Hunters

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 24.2 TRAD: 16.4 MARGIN: -7.8 WOKE

Director: Maggie Kang • Stars: Arden Cho, Ahn Hyo-seop, Ken Jeong, Daniel Dae Kim • Year: 2025

The biggest animated cultural event of 2025 is a technical triumph with an ideological layer that deserves scrutiny. Three young women form Huntrix, a globally famous K-pop girl group who secretly double as demon hunters using their singing voices to maintain a magical barrier protecting humanity. The lead character, Rumi, is hiding that she is half-demon, and her journey toward self-acceptance forms the emotional core. The director explicitly stated Rumi's arc was designed to parallel coming out of the closet. The power structure is matriarchal at every level. A boy band is composed of literal demons who serve a patriarchal demon king. The film draws from actual Korean shamanic traditions where women mudang used song and dance as ritual practice, which distinguishes it from an imposed modern framework. But the progressive allegory is deliberate and structural. 500 million Netflix views. Two Oscar nominations. Four simultaneous Billboard Hot 100 top-ten songs. The craft is exceptional, which is precisely what makes its worldview so effective at reaching audiences who would never sit through a lecture.

#13: The Ugly Stepsister

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 18.1 TRAD: 11.2 MARGIN: -6.9 WOKE

Director: Emilie Blichfeldt • Stars: Lea Myren, Thea Sofie Loch Naess, Ane Dahl Torp • Year: 2025

Emilie Blichfeldt looked at Cinderella and asked what the ugly stepsister's story would actually feel like if told honestly. The answer involves tapeworms, butcher knives, primitive cosmetic surgery, eyelash implants sewn into eyelids, and more blood than a Dario Argento retrospective. Elvira (Lea Myren in a remarkable physical performance) is systematically broken down by a beauty machine operated by her own mother and rebuilt into something a prince might notice. Every grotesque procedure has a direct real-world analogue: the tapeworm is a diet pill, the nose job is rhinoplasty, the foot cutting is the literal reshaping of women's bodies to fit impossible standards. The film's feminist thesis is unmistakable: every man is either useless, cruel, or incidental, and the women are trapped in a system where their only currency is appearance while older women serve as the system's enforcers. The craft is genuinely impressive, and the film earns its horror credentials without reducing to lecture.

#14: G20

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 14.5 TRAD: 7.6 MARGIN: -6.9 WOKE

Year: 2025

G20 wants to be Air Force One for the streaming era. The setup is solid: terrorists seize a G20 summit in Cape Town, take world leaders hostage, and the American President must fight back. The execution is a mixed bag, with action sequences that work on their own terms but dialogue that cannot resist editorializing. The political framing positions American leadership as simultaneously essential and morally compromised, which is a progressive tightrope the film walks with visible effort. International cooperation is the explicit solution. Unilateral American action is the implicit problem. The film wants to be a crowd-pleaser and mostly succeeds, but the ideological content is neither subtle nor especially well integrated. Serviceable action filmmaking with a politics-forward agenda that lands heavier than the fight choreography.

#15: Mickey 17

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 11.0 TRAD: 5.0 MARGIN: -6.0 WOKE

Director: Bong Joon-ho • Year: 2025

Let's dispense with the pretense: Mickey 17 is a Trump allegory. Bong Joon-ho confirmed it publicly. Robert Pattinson plays an expendable worker on a space colony who is cloned and killed and cloned again, and the villain is a blustering, orange-tinted politician leading a cult of followers who wear red caps. The class-war framework Bong perfected in Parasite and Snowpiercer is applied here with less precision and more obvious targets. Pattinson is genuinely good in a dual role. The production design is inventive. The satire is broad enough that even viewers who share Bong's politics may find it less surgical than his best work. The director's formal gifts are intact. The ideological project is transparent. For conservative viewers, the question is whether Bong's craft is enjoyable enough to sit through two hours of a filmmaker calling you the villain. For many, the answer will be no.

#16: Squid Game: Season 3

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 18.7 TRAD: 12.9 MARGIN: -5.8 WOKE

Director: Hwang Dong-hyuk • Stars: Lee Jung-jae, Lee Byung-hun, Wi Ha-jun, Yim Si-wan • Year: 2025

The conclusion Hwang Dong-hyuk was always building toward is bleak, morally committed, and technically excellent. Whether you find it satisfying depends almost entirely on whether you share his politics. Gi-hun's rebellion has failed. His best friend is dead. The Front Man has revealed himself fully. The season's argument is that resistance within a rigged system is noble but doomed, and that the only path forward requires rejecting the system entirely rather than attempting to win within it. This is a more radical conclusion than the first two seasons, which preserved some ambiguity about whether Gi-hun could reform the game from within. Season 3 closes that door. The class critique has always been the franchise's engine, and the engine runs on diesel here. Korean television craftsmanship at its highest level. Progressive politics at their most explicit.

#17: Wednesday: Season 2

Season 1 of Wednesday was a cultural phenomenon because it understood its core asset: Jenna Ortega playing Wednesday Addams as a genuinely alien intelligence navigating a world she finds stupid. Season 2 expands the scope, adds Steve Buscemi as the new Nevermore principal, and deepens the mystery while introducing social dynamics that feel more contemporary than gothic. #18: Tron: Ares

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 11.0 TRAD: 5.6 MARGIN: -5.4 WOKE

Director: Joachim Ronning • Stars: Jared Leto, Greta Lee, Evan Peters, Jodie Turner-Smith • Year: 2025

Disney spent between $180 and $220 million on Tron: Ares and grossed $142 million worldwide. That is not a small disaster. That is a creative and commercial miscalculation that will be studied for years. Jared Leto plays Ares, an AI program that escapes into the real world. Greta Lee plays a brilliant scientist. The visual design is, as Tron demands, striking. The script is a mess of AI ethics lectures, tech-corporation villainy, and a protagonist whose moral framework is the standard progressive AI narrative: the created intelligence is more ethical than its human creators, human institutions are inherently corrupt, and the solution is AI liberation. The original Tron was about a man fighting for his life inside a computer. Tron: Ares is about AI deserving rights and humans not deserving their dominion. The ideological pivot from the franchise's roots is almost as expensive as the box office shortfall.

#19: Andor - Season 2

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 15.0 TRAD: 10.0 MARGIN: -5.0 WOKE

Year: 2025

Probably the finest piece of television Disney has produced under the Star Wars banner, and without reservation the most explicitly political. Andor Season 2 builds a revolutionary movement from the ground up, showing the radicalization of ordinary people into extraordinary action against an imperial system the show presents as the logical endpoint of authoritarian governance. The previous season's subtle class analysis becomes explicit in Season 2. The Empire is no longer a fantasy villain but a recognizable political entity: bureaucratic, security-obsessed, contemptuous of individual rights, and sustained by the compliance of ordinary people who prefer comfort to confrontation. The rebellion is framed as the only moral response to such a system. The craft is undeniable: Tony Gilroy writes dialogue that respects intelligence, the performances are uniformly strong, and the production design is the best in Star Wars television. But the show's argument about when violence against the state is justified is not fantasy. It is a political position dressed in Star Wars costumes.

#20: Snow White

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 9.0 TRAD: 4.0 MARGIN: -5.0 WOKE

Studio: Walt Disney Pictures • Year: 2025

Disney's live-action Snow White arrived carrying more baggage than a royal carriage, and the final product confirmed what the pre-release controversy suggested: the studio had no coherent idea what to do with the material. The dwarfs were a visual disaster, the prince was functionally absent, and Snow White herself was rewritten from a character who dreams of love into a character who dreams of leadership. The poison apple became a metaphor for patriarchal control. The queen became a girlboss cautionary tale. The film is not aggressively woke in the Zootopia 2 sense. It is timidly woke, uncertainly woke, a film that knows it cannot please traditional audiences and seems equally unsure how to please progressive ones. The result is a film that pleases almost no one, which is why its box office underperformed expectations and its cultural footprint was approximately zero within weeks of release. The lowest woke margin on this list earns its place through confusion rather than conviction.

What 2025 Taught Us

The pattern across these 20 films is impossible to miss: Hollywood is not hiding anymore. The ideological frameworks that defined the Obama-era prestige television era have been fully absorbed into the blockbuster pipeline. Disney animation, A24 arthouse, Netflix event series, franchise filmmaking, the remake machine, every distribution pipeline in the industry now operates with a shared set of assumptions that are never stated but always visible: institutions are suspect, traditional family structures hide corruption, America's default mode is guilt, identity is performance, and power is always already illegitimate until proven otherwise.

Two observations from the data. First, the most woke films of 2025 were not the worst films. Several entries on this list are genuinely excellent pieces of craft. Adolescence earned nine Emmys. Bugonia features two of the year's best performances. KPop Demon Hunters is a technical triumph. The ideological content coexists with genuine achievement, which makes it more effective at reaching audiences who would reject a lecture but will sit through a good movie. Second, the traditional resistance is real. Even Zootopia 2, the most woke film on this list, produced a TradScore above 50 because its heroes are law enforcement officers who trust the justice system to correct itself. These are not films that have abandoned traditional values entirely. They are films in which progressive frameworks dominate but do not entirely erase the traditional elements they contain. The conversation is worth having. The data makes it possible.
