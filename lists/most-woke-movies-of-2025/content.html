2025 was the year the ideological floodgates opened all the way. Every genre, every platform, every demographic. Animated children's films about systemic exclusion and colonial theft. Horror films that argue conservative small towns are the real monsters. Prestige dramas that turn therapy culture into narrative structure. Blockbuster franchises that cannot stop preaching between explosions. Streaming series that treat identity politics as the emotional centerpiece. A24 distributing at least three films on this list with pride.

We reviewed hundreds of films and shows in 2025. This list ranks the 25 most ideologically aggressive among them, from most woke to least. Every entry links to the full VirtueVigil review with complete scoring methodology. The margin, tradScore minus wokeScore, reflects how thoroughly progressive ideology dominates the work. A -38 margin means the film's woke content overwhelms its traditional content by 38 points on our 100-point dual scale. These are not close calls.

What makes 2025 distinctive: the ideology crossed deeper into family entertainment than ever before. Half this list is PG or PG-13. The year's most celebrated animated film (Zootopia 2) is also its most woke. Netflix alone contributed seven entries. Horror, traditionally a genre where conservative audiences could find refuge from politics, produced four of the top ten. The walls between entertainment and advocacy did not just thin in 2025. They collapsed. Ranked from most woke to least woke.

#1 (Most Woke): Zootopia 2 (2025)

STRONGLY WOKE MARGIN: -38 WOKE

Genre: Animation • Platform: Theatrical

Zootopia 2 is exactly what it looks like. Disney's animated buddy-cop sequel takes the original's progressive allegory and turns every dial to maximum. Where the first Zootopia tackled prejudice and governing through fear, the sequel targets systemic exclusion, historical revisionism, and wealth built on theft and oppression — delivered through talking animals in a gorgeous animated world. The villain is a wealthy founding family whose dynasty was built on stealing a reptile inventor's work, framing her for murder, and exiling an entire population. Zootopia's prosperity itself is exposed as a lie built on colonial displacement. At -38, Zootopia 2 is the most woke film of 2025 and the second-most woke film in our entire database of over 700 reviews. It is marketed to children. Parents who dismissed the first film's messaging as background noise will find no such comfort here.

#2: Bugonia (2025)

STRONGLY WOKE MARGIN: -20 WOKE

Genre: Black Comedy/Sci-Fi Thriller • Platform: Theatrical

Yorgos Lanthimos delivers another ideological sledgehammer, this time wrapping a radical anti-capitalist, environmentalist manifesto inside a genre-bending alien conspiracy kidnapping story. Jesse Plemons and Aidan Delbis play conspiracy-obsessed men who kidnap a pharmaceutical CEO (Emma Stone), convinced she is an alien overlord responsible for killing Earth's honeybees. The film's final twist reveals the CEO actually is an alien — and after surveying humanity's record of violence, environmental destruction, and corporate greed, she calmly annihilates every human on Earth. The message is not subtle: humans are a failed experiment, capitalism is a death machine, and the planet would be better off without us. Stone earned an Oscar nomination for the role. The craft is undeniable. So is the misanthropy.

#3: Clown in a Cornfield (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -18 WOKE

Genre: Horror • Platform: Theatrical (RLJE Films / Shudder)

A competently made slasher that conceals its political agenda until past the midpoint and then delivers it with zero ambiguity. For about fifty-five minutes, Clown in a Cornfield works as the genre film it claims to be: teenagers getting killed by a mascot-costumed murderer in a dying Missouri town. Then the masks come off — the killers are the town establishment, the mayor, sheriff, teacher, shopkeeper, and diner waitress, who have organized a coordinated murder campaign against the young. The mayor burned down the town's economic foundation himself and blamed the teenagers. The generational-warfare allegory is unmistakable: older conservatives are literally murdering the young to preserve their dying world. At -18, it earns its score by using the second half of a slasher film to argue the real horror is conservative small-town America.

#4: The Wedding Banquet (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -15 WOKE

Genre: Romantic Comedy • Platform: Theatrical (Bleecker Street)

The original 1993 Wedding Banquet took the parents seriously — they were not villains, just people whose happiness depended on their son's silence. The 2025 remake makes a different choice. It starts from a fully visible queer household complete with a PFLAG-active mother (Joan Chen), and rebuilds the farce around a visa problem rather than closeted identity. #5: Eddington (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -15 WOKE

Genre: Western / Dark Comedy / Thriller • Platform: Theatrical

Ari Aster's most ambitious film argues that your worldview is a disease. Joaquin Phoenix plays a small-town New Mexico sheriff whose journey from decent lawman to conspiratorial extremist during COVID is presented not as individual failure but as cultural inevitability — the conservative community's distrust of expertise and institutions is the ideal medium for extremist growth. Phoenix delivers the finest performance of his career since The Master, and it is wasted on an ideological project. The film does not engage with the genuine institutional failures of the pandemic era; it presents institutional distrust as pathology without acknowledging the failures that created reasonable grounds for it. At -15, Eddington is technically masterful and formally extraordinary filmmaking that argues your epistemology produces dangerous outcomes.

#6: Now You See Me: Now You Don't (2025)

#7: Presence (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -10 WOKE

Genre: Horror/Thriller • Platform: Theatrical

Steven Soderbergh's most technically inventive horror film is shot entirely from the ghost's POV — the camera drifts through rooms, watches from doorways, hovers protectively over the family's neglected daughter Chloe. The formal innovation is genuine. The problem is what the ghost is saying. The presence sides exclusively with Chloe, the sensitive daughter who sees her family clearly, and is explicitly hostile to everyone else: it destroys the son's room when he catfishes a classmate, closes Chloe's door to give her privacy, and validates her emotional reality against every other family member. The ambitious mother is wrong about everything. The passive father is kind but useless. At -10, Presence frames parental authority and traditional family structure as the villain, with a supernatural force serving as the film's progressive moral arbiter.

#10: Avatar: Fire and Ash (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -8 WOKE LEAN

Genre: Epic Sci-Fi Action Adventure • Platform: Theatrical

James Cameron's third Avatar is both the best-looking film of 2025 and an almost proud repetition of every idea the franchise put on the table in 2009. The anti-military messaging, noble-indigenous-versus-corporate-colonizers framework, and white protagonist who becomes the best version of the culture he joined — Cameron has been making this film since Aliens and does not apologize for it. #11: KPop Demon Hunters (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -8 WOKE LEAN

The year's most interesting ideological artifact: a genuinely brilliant animated musical with 500 million Netflix views, two Oscar nominations, and four simultaneous Billboard Hot 100 top-ten songs, built on a framework that blends authentic Korean shamanic tradition with contemporary progressive identity politics. #12: Adolescence (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -8 WOKE LEAN

Genre: Psychological Crime Drama • Platform: Netflix

The most important piece of television produced in 2025 — and that is not the same as saying it is the best. Over four episodes, each shot in a single unbroken take, Adolescence examines the arrest of a 13-year-old boy for murdering a classmate, the school's response, his psychological evaluation, and his family's collapse. The one-take format is the show's thesis expressed through form: you cannot cut away, fast-forward, or take a breath. Stephen Graham is extraordinary, but the show adopts a clear progressive framework: toxic online masculinity as the cause of violence, with the manosphere and internet radicalization as the villains. At -8, Adolescence has already been claimed by both sides as evidence for their worldview. The show itself is better than the discourse it generated — which is exactly what makes its perspective so persuasive and so worth scrutinizing.

#19: Tron: Ares (2025)

Cost Disney between $180 and $220 million and grossed $142 million worldwide. #22: Snow White (2025)

Disney's live-action Snow White arrives carrying more baggage than a royal carriage. #23: Stranger Things (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -5 WOKE LEAN

Genre: Sci-Fi & Fantasy • Platform: Netflix

Stranger Things is the definitive long-form woke trap. Season 1 was a near-perfect piece of television from a traditional values perspective: Joyce Byers collapsing into a primal mission to find her son, Jim Hopper channeling grief into protecting someone else's child, boys being brave and loyal and resourceful in the way adventure stories used to be. Then came Season 3: Robin Buckley comes out as a lesbian. Season 4 leans harder into Robin's sexuality and Will's unspoken feelings for Mike. By the final season, Will's coming-out scene is the emotional centerpiece, his acceptance of his gay identity literally unlocks telekinetic superpowers, and the military and government are the real villains. At -5, Stranger Things is a lesson in how long-form storytelling can be weaponized — you invest in the characters, you trust the world, and then season by season, the world changes around you until you are watching a completely different show than the one you signed up for.
