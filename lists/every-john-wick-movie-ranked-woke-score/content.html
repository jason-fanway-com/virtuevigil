No major Hollywood franchise has resisted the ideological tide of the last decade as cleanly as John Wick. While other long-running series spent their sequels inserting lectures, deconstructing their heroes, and apologizing for their own genre, Chad Stahelski and Keanu Reeves built something rare: an action universe that treats honor, competence, and consequence as real moral values, and refuses to apologize for any of them. Five films. One code. And the numbers prove it.

VirtueVigil has reviewed all five entries in the John Wick universe, from the 2014 original through the 2025 spinoff Ballerina. Every single one scores in traditional territory. Four of five land at TRADITIONAL or stronger. Only Ballerina, the female-led spinoff, slips to a TRADITIONAL LEAN, and even that film never crosses into woke ground. No other major action franchise in our database holds a cleaner values profile.

The reason is structural. John Wick's world runs on honor codes that are older and more binding than any law. The Continental Hotel is neutral ground. Markers are blood oaths. Violations have immediate, non-negotiable consequences. This is not a universe that questions whether rules are worth having. It is a universe built on the premise that rules are what keep chaos at bay, and the men who keep their word are the ones who survive. That is a conservative moral architecture, and it is the load-bearing wall of every film on this list.

VirtueVigil uses a dual-scoring system that measures traditional values content and progressive ideological content independently. These are not quality ratings. A higher traditional score means the film rewards discipline, loyalty, sacrifice, and earned competence. A higher woke score means it smuggles in progressive messaging, often hidden behind genre. The list below runs from most traditional at the top to the franchise's least traditional entry at the bottom. Every film links to its full VirtueVigil review.

Five films. One code. This is what the numbers say about Hollywood's cleanest major franchise.

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL

#1. John Wick: Chapter 4 (2023)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 23.5 WOKE: 0.5 MARGIN: +23 TRAD

Director: Chad Stahelski | Stars: Keanu Reeves, Ian McShane, Lance Reddick, Donnie Yen | Year: 2023

John Wick: Chapter 4 is the rare action sequel that earns the word epic. Chad Stahelski's nearly three-hour opus scales everything that made the first film work to operatic proportions without losing the personal stakes at its core. A woke score of 0.5. A traditional score of 23.5. That is the cleanest values profile of any major action franchise in our database, and it is not close. The film's internal moral universe is more coherent than most dramas. John Wick does what he does because he made promises to people he loves, and he keeps them. That is the whole movie.

The film never once asks you to feel bad about enjoying violence, and it never once pauses to deliver a lecture. The honor codes of the High Table, the inviolable sanctuary of the Continental, the markers and blood oaths that bind killers to their word, all of it is treated as sacred architecture rather than something to be deconstructed. The 0.5 woke score reflects a film with essentially zero progressive ideological content. This is the franchise at its most confident, and the numbers back up what the audience already knew: John Wick is one of the best action films made in the last decade, and the cleanest major franchise from a traditional values standpoint.

#2. John Wick: Chapter 2 (2017)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 22.5 WOKE: 2.1 MARGIN: +20 TRAD

Director: Chad Stahelski | Stars: Keanu Reeves, Riccardo Scamarcio, Common, Ian McShane | Year: 2017

Chapter 2 is a better film than it has any business being. The original was a stripped-down revenge story that worked on pure execution. This sequel expands the mythology while retaining the thing that made the first film worth watching: an absolute commitment to the idea that honor, even honor among killers, means something. The premise hinges on a marker, a blood oath Wick made years ago to a Roman crime lord. Santino calls it in. Wick is compelled to travel to Rome and assassinate Santino's sister so Santino can claim the High Table seat she holds. The premise is clean: Wick made a promise. He is a man of his word. He hates this, but he does it.

The film's traditional argument is made concrete in every frame. The High Table and the Continental are a world with functioning honor codes, inviolable sanctuary rules, and real consequences for breaking them. Santino's problem is not that he is evil. It is that he thinks rules are for other people. The system responds accordingly, and immediately, and without appeal. The woke content is minimal to the point of irrelevance. Ruby Rose plays a mute female assassin and Common plays a peer assassin, and neither casting decision carries ideological freight. They are simply characters in a world full of characters. A 2.1 woke score and a 22.5 traditional score is what a franchise looks like when it expands its world without shrinking its story.

TRADITIONAL

#3. John Wick: Chapter 3 - Parabellum (2019)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 22.5 WOKE: 6.1 MARGIN: +16 TRAD

Director: Chad Stahelski | Stars: Keanu Reeves, Halle Berry, Ian McShane, Laurence Fishburne | Year: 2019

Parabellum opens exactly where Chapter 2 ended: John Wick is running. He has one hour before excommunicado takes effect, before every assassin in the Continental network has a $14 million bounty and legal license to kill him. The film does not pause for the audience to settle in. It starts moving and does not stop for 130 minutes. The action is the best in the franchise up to this point, from the opening library fight to the glass-house finale, but the values are what earn it a place on this list.

John Wick's world runs on honor. Not sentiment, not politics. Honor. You keep your word. You pay your debts. You accept the consequences of the rules you break. Wick is not fighting the High Table because it is evil. He is fighting it because he wants to survive, and the distinction matters. The film's woke content is real but contained. Asia Kate Dillon's Adjudicator, a non-binary functionary dispensing punishments with austere efficiency, is a mild signal honestly presented, not hidden or amplified into a message. Halle Berry's Sofia is a capable fighter in a franchise that has always included competent female assassins. Neither is the film's ideology. The ideology remains fundamentally traditional, and the 6.1 woke score against 22.5 traditional reflects exactly that balance.

#4. John Wick (2014)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 15.3 WOKE: 0.7 MARGIN: +15 TRAD

Director: Chad Stahelski | Stars: Keanu Reeves, Ian McShane, Willem Dafoe, Adrianne Palicki | Year: 2014

The original is the purest action film of the 21st century. Not because it is complex, but because it has total clarity of purpose and executes that purpose with absolute technical excellence. A man's wife dies. She leaves him a puppy so he will have something to grieve alongside her. Russian mobsters break into his home and kill the puppy. He returns to the criminal underworld he left for love, and he kills everyone responsible. There is no ambiguity about whether John Wick is justified. The film makes a clean moral calculation: some men deserve what they get.

The masculine warrior ethos is central, authentic, and celebrated without qualification. John Wick is the Baba Yaga, the boogeyman, the man you send to kill the boogeyman. His reputation is built on competence, discipline, and keeping his word, not on cruelty. The film's moral engine runs on grief and loyalty: he left the life for love, and the inciting incident is an act of grief desecrated. The woke-adjacent content is minimal. Ms. Perkins, the female assassin who betrays the Continental's code, is not a feminist symbol. She is a professional who makes a bad decision and pays the price, exactly as any male character would. A 0.7 woke score against 15.3 traditional is the template everything that followed built on.

TRADITIONAL LEAN

#5. From the World of John Wick: Ballerina (2025)

TRADITIONAL LEAN TRAD: 18.8 WOKE: 11.3 MARGIN: +7 TRAD

Director: Len Wiseman | Stars: Ana de Armas, Gabriel Byrne, Norman Reedus, Keanu Reeves | Year: 2025

Ballerina is the franchise's least traditional entry, and even it never crosses into woke ground. The most important thing conservative audiences need to hear: this is not a hostile takeover of the John Wick franchise. Eve Macarro is not replacing John Wick. She is operating in his world, with his help, and the film treats Keanu Reeves' character with complete deference. There is no feminist lecture. There is no moment where Eve tells a man he is part of the problem. She just wants to kill the people who murdered her father. That is it.

The woke content is real but restrained. The film acknowledges Eve's physical disadvantage against larger opponents, then mostly ignores it in favor of brute persistence, the familiar accommodating-stuntmen problem of female-led action. The Emerald Fennell script polish generated more heat than the final product warrants. But the family values throughline is strong. Eve's entire motivation is avenging her father. Norman Reedus' character exists to protect his daughter. The Ruska Roma functions as a surrogate family. Strip away the gun fu and the body count, and Ballerina is a movie about what people will do to protect or avenge the people they love. A 11.3 woke score against 18.8 traditional lands it at a TRADITIONAL LEAN, a clear step down from the mainline films but a world away from what most major franchises do with a female-led spinoff.

The Big Picture

Five John Wick films reviewed. Five traditional scores. Zero woke scores. By the raw numbers, the franchise is 100 percent traditional in VirtueVigil's database. That is not an accident. It is the direct result of a creative team that decided early what kind of story it was telling and refused to drift.

The four mainline films average a margin of roughly +18.5 traditional, with a combined woke score so low it barely registers. The woke signals that do exist, the Adjudicator in Chapter 3, Ruby Rose in Chapter 2, are mild, honestly presented, and never elevated into the film's thesis. The franchise's traditional architecture, honor codes, markers, sanctuary rules, consequences for betrayal, is the load-bearing wall of every single entry. When Santino breaks sanctuary in Chapter 2, the system mobilizes against him immediately. When Wick breaks the rules in Chapter 3, he pays for it in blood. The films never present this as unjust. They present it as necessary. A world with rules that mean nothing is a world that collapses.

Compare this to what other franchises did in the same decade. The MCU spent Phase Four and Five inserting identity politics into nearly every release. Star Wars turned its flagship Sequel Trilogy centerpiece into a deconstruction of its own mythology. John Wick did none of that. It kept its hero a man of few words and absolute competence, kept its women as capable professionals rather than symbols, and kept its moral engine running on grief, loyalty, and the justice of consequences. The audience noticed. Every film in the franchise outgrossed the one before it, and Chapter 4 became the rare fourth entry that critics and audiences agreed was the best of the series.

Ballerina is the one data point that gives a parent pause, and only mildly. It is a competent, entertaining revenge thriller that respects the franchise and never tries to be anything other than what it is. Conservative viewers worried about John Wick being sidelined or humiliated can relax. He is treated as the legend he is. He just is not the main character this time. The franchise's future will hinge on whether future spinoffs hold that same discipline, or whether the success of Ballerina invites more of the accommodating-stuntmen girl boss energy that quietly crept into its back half. For now, the record is clean.
