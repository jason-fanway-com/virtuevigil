Hulu occupies an interesting position in the streaming landscape. It is not the biggest platform, not the most prestigious, and not the one parents think about first when they worry about what their kids are watching. But the VirtueVigil database now holds 17 Hulu originals with completed Woke Scores, and the pattern that emerges is worth understanding: Hulu's original programming leans more traditional than most of its competitors, but what it does put on the woke side is among the most ideologically aggressive content on any platform.

The spread is dramatic. At the top sits The Handmaid's Tale at a -26.2 margin (STRONGLY WOKE). At the bottom sits The Bear Season 2 at a +30.7 margin (STRONGLY TRADITIONAL). That is a 57-point range across just 17 titles. The Bear franchise alone occupies five slots on this list, and watching its ideological arc across seasons is one of the most instructive case studies in how prestige television's values have shifted.

The shape of the distribution tells the story. Two titles land STRONGLY WOKE, three more in the woke tiers, and then a rapid transition: twelve of the seventeen titles lean traditional or stronger. Hulu's programming instincts, whether by accident or design, produce more traditional content than woke content by volume. But the woke titles it does produce are among the most ideologically explicit shows on any streaming service. The Handmaid's Tale and Immaculate are not subtle. They are manifestos.

For parents, the actionable insight is this: Hulu is safer than Netflix for family viewing, but the progressive content it does carry is in a league of its own for ideological intensity. Know which titles those are. Here is every Hulu original in our database, ranked from most woke to most traditional.

STRONGLY WOKE (Margin -20.0 and Below)

#1: The Handmaid's Tale (2017)

Woke Score: 30.2 • Traditional Score: 4.0 • Verdict: STRONGLY WOKE • Margin: -26.2

Genre: Dystopian Drama | Platform: Hulu | Director: Reed Morano (pilot)

The Handmaid's Tale is one of the most openly ideological shows ever to reach a mass American audience, and it deserves to be understood as exactly that. It is not a neutral portrait of a dystopia. It is a polemic, and it is effective precisely because it wraps its political argument in genuinely powerful filmmaking and extraordinary performances. Elisabeth Moss carries the series on her shoulders, and the production design creates a world that feels nauseatingly real. But the show's worldview is unambiguous: traditional religious values are a mask for patriarchal oppression, and the only moral response is resistance. At a -26.2 margin, this is the most woke title on Hulu by a significant margin, and one of the most ideologically aggressive shows on any platform.

#2: Immaculate (2024)

Woke Score: 25.4 • Traditional Score: 3.5 • Verdict: WOKE • Margin: -21.9

Genre: Horror | Platform: Hulu

Sydney Sweeney spent years developing Immaculate as a vehicle that would let her operate in the horror genre while carrying a message she clearly believed in. The film that emerged, shot in Italy, draped in Catholic imagery, and centered on a nun who becomes mysteriously pregnant, is a religious horror that uses the Church as its villain. The traditional score is almost nonexistent because the film's view of organized religion is fundamentally hostile. This is not a nuanced critique. It is a horror movie where the institution itself is the monster, and the ending makes that point with shocking, unforgettable clarity. At -21.9, this is a WOKE verdict with STRONGLY WOKE intensity.

Furious arrived on Hulu with a 100% Rotten Tomatoes score and a premise that reads like an algorithm trained on the post-MeToo, post-Epstein zeitgeist. #6: Scrubs - Season 10 Revival (2026)

Woke Score: 11.4 • Traditional Score: 8.4 • Verdict: TRADITIONAL • Margin: -3.0

The original Scrubs ran nine seasons across NBC and ABC, and at its best it was something genuinely special: a workplace comedy that balanced absurdist fantasy with real emotional stakes. The woke score is elevated by the revival's insistence on updating the show's sensibility for modern audiences, complete with new character dynamics and thematic preoccupations that the original never carried. Fans of the original will find echoes of what they loved. They will also find a show that has been re-tuned for a different era, and the scoring reflects exactly how much.

Genre: Horror / Sci-Fi / Thriller | Platform: Hulu | Showrunner: Noah Hawley

The Alien franchise has been waiting for someone to bring the xenomorph to Earth in a serious way, and Noah Hawley is exactly the kind of cerebral showrunner you want for that job. Alien: Earth is tense, atmospheric, and genuinely frightening in stretches. #8: The Americans (2013)

Woke Score: 11.4 • Traditional Score: 16.6 • Verdict: TRADITIONAL LEAN • Margin: +5.0

Genre: Spy Thriller / Period Drama | Platform: Hulu | Creator: Joe Weisberg

The Americans is one of the finest television dramas of the 2010s, and it presents a uniquely challenging case for ideological scoring. The premise alone raises eyebrows: two KGB officers, Elizabeth and Philip Jennings, pose as a married American couple operating a travel agency in suburban DC while running espionage operations for the Soviet Union. In less capable hands this would be a progressive fever dream. But Joe Weisberg, a former CIA officer, treats both sides with unusual complexity. The Jennings' marriage becomes real, their love for their American-born children becomes real, and the show's ultimate argument, that human bonds transcend political ideology, is fundamentally traditional. The woke score registers the show's willingness to humanize Soviet agents and critique American institutions. The traditional score registers what the show is actually about.

#11: Prey (2022)

Prey is the best Predator film since the 1987 original, and it achieves that by doing something genuinely smart: stripping the franchise down to its essential elements and relocating them to the Comanche Nation in 1719. The result is a lean, ferocious survival thriller that works on its own terms. The traditional score is robust because the film's core is about a young woman proving herself through competence, courage, and sacrifice. The woke score registers because the film cannot resist framing her struggle against patriarchal tribal norms, and the colonialist subtext is never far from the surface. But the film earns its BALANCED TRADITIONAL verdict by letting action and character, not sermonizing, carry the weight. Naru wins because she is smarter and tougher, not because the movie is making a point.

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL (Margin +20.0 and Above)

#16: Man on Fire (2004)

Woke Score: 1.4 • Traditional Score: 27.6 • Verdict: STRONGLY TRADITIONAL • Margin: +26.2

Genre: Action / Thriller | Platform: Hulu | Director: Tony Scott

Man on Fire is one of the purest traditional value systems in American cinema, delivered at 146 minutes of operatic vengeance. The whole film runs on a single premise: protecting the innocent is not just a job, it is a sacred obligation, and failing to protect them demands a response that no legal system can provide. Denzel Washington's John Creasy is a broken man who finds redemption through his relationship with a child he is hired to protect. When she is taken, his response is absolute. The woke score is essentially zero because Tony Scott has no interest in complicating the moral arithmetic. Evil exists. It must be destroyed. The cost to the destroyer is irrelevant. This is not a film interested in nuance, and that is exactly what makes it one of the most ideologically coherent traditional films in the Hulu catalog.

#17: The Bear (Season 2) (2023)

Woke Score: 4.2 • Traditional Score: 34.9 • Verdict: STRONGLY TRADITIONAL • Margin: +30.7

Genre: Comedy-Drama | Platform: Hulu

The Bear Season 2 is the story of what happens when people who have been surviving decide to build something instead. That turns out to be a more traditional story than Season 1, and a richer one. The season follows the team as they tear down the old beef sandwich shop and construct a fine-dining restaurant from scratch, and every character arc is built on the same foundation: growth requires discipline, excellence requires sacrifice, and you cannot build something meaningful without learning to trust the people building it with you. The traditional score of 34.9 is one of the highest on Hulu because the season's entire architecture is conservative in the deepest sense. It is about creation, not critique. About what you owe to the people who depend on you, not what you are owed by the systems around you. And in the season's best episode, "Forks," Richie Jerimovich learns that service is not servitude. It is the highest form of dignity. That is not a progressive idea. It might be the most traditional idea in television.
