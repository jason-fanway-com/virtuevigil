The Western is America's founding myth. It is the story of the frontier: of law and lawlessness, of civilization pushing against wilderness, of men and women making something from nothing in a land that does not care whether they live or die. For half a century, the Western was Hollywood's dominant genre. Then it became Hollywood's favorite genre to deconstruct, rewrite, and apologize for. The result is a genre that tells you as much about the year it was made as the year it depicts.

The VirtueVigil database now holds 19 Western movies and television shows with completed Woke Scores, and the spread they reveal is remarkable. At one end sits Westworld at a -20.4 margin (STRONGLY WOKE), a show that uses its Western setting as a backdrop for a full-throated critique of human nature, capitalism, and every institution the frontier was supposed to build. At the other end sits Horizon: An American Saga - Chapter 1 at a +22.6 margin (TRADITIONAL) and Breaking Bad at +25.9, works that take the Western code seriously: individual agency, moral consequence, and the hard choices that define a life.

The pattern is clear and instructive. The older the Western, the more likely it is to score traditional. The revisionist Westerns of the last two decades are almost entirely woke or mixed. The neo-Westerns of the streaming era are a battleground, with some shows embracing traditional frontier values and others using the Western setting as a Trojan horse for contemporary ideological messaging. Parents who want to know what their families are absorbing when they watch a Western need to see the numbers. Here they are, ranked from most woke to most traditional.

STRONGLY WOKE (Margin -20.0 and Below)

#1: Westworld (2016)

Woke Score: 25.3 • Traditional Score: 4.9 • Verdict: STRONGLY WOKE • Margin: -20.4

Genre: Science Fiction, Western, Drama, Dystopian | Platform: HBO

Westworld is the most woke Western title in the database because it is not really a Western at all. It is a science fiction show about a Western-themed amusement park, and the park is a metaphor for exploitation, consciousness, and the sins of those who build civilizations on the backs of others. The 25.3 woke score reflects a show that is more interested in deconstructing the Western myth than in telling one. The frontier setting is a costume the show puts on characters it intends to strip naked and interrogate. The 4.9 traditional score reflects a show that has almost nothing to say about the actual values of the frontier: courage, self-reliance, or the hard-won order that follows chaos. It is prestige television built on the premise that the Western is something to be ashamed of, and the numbers bear that out.

WOKE (Margin -10.0 to -19.9)

#2: Eddington (2025)

Woke Score: 23.7 • Traditional Score: 8.2 • Verdict: WOKE • Margin: -15.5

Genre: Western / Dark Comedy / Thriller | Platform: Theatrical | Director: Ari Aster

Ari Aster brought his signature brand of psychological horror to the Western genre and produced something that looks like a Western but thinks like a progressive indictment of small-town America. Eddington uses its desert setting to argue that isolation breeds pathology, that the frontier dream curdles into paranoia and cruelty, and that the people who retreat from society are not pioneers but threats. The 23.7 woke score is high because the film is relentless in its thesis. The 8.2 traditional score exists because Aster is a craftsman who builds real tension and real stakes, which is precisely what makes his ideological framing more effective, not less.

MIXED (Margin 0.0 to +9.9)

#3: Brokeback Mountain (2005)

Woke Score: 18.0 • Traditional Score: 18.0 • Verdict: MIXED • Margin: 0.0

Genre: Romantic Drama, Neo-Western | Platform: Peacock / Digital Rental | Director: Ang Lee

Brokeback Mountain lands at exactly 0.0, a dead-even split between woke and traditional content, and that is the fairest possible verdict on a film that is genuinely two things at once. The woke score comes from its subject matter: a romantic relationship between two men in a genre and a setting that has historically been the most masculine and heterosexual in American cinema. The traditional score comes from everything else: the film's treatment of loyalty, sacrifice, family obligation, and the grief that comes from honoring commitments even when they cost you everything. Brokeback Mountain is a portrait of men who try to do the right thing and fail in ways that destroy them, which is as traditional a Western theme as any ever written. It is also, undeniably, a film about a love that the culture of its time could not accommodate. The equal scores reflect a film that earns its ambiguity.

#4: No Country for Old Men (2007)

Woke Score: 7.1 • Traditional Score: 11.3 • Verdict: MIXED • Margin: +4.2

Genre: Crime / Thriller / Drama / Neo-Western | Platform: Theatrical | Director: Joel Coen, Ethan Coen

The Coen Brothers' masterpiece is a Western in setting and spirit, but its worldview is harder to classify. The woke score is modest: the film has no interest in identity politics and no progressive thesis to advance. What it has is a deep, almost nihilistic ambivalence about whether order can prevail against chaos, which is not woke but is also not traditionally affirming. The traditional score is driven by Sheriff Bell's genuine moral seriousness and the film's unblinking portrait of consequences. Actions have results in No Country for Old Men; there is no redemption arc and no therapeutic resolution, just the weight of what was done and what was lost. That is the frontier as the Coens see it, and it lands MIXED because it is too honest to fit either camp.

#7: Django Unchained (2012)

Woke Score: 13.4 • Traditional Score: 21.5 • Verdict: MIXED • Margin: +8.1

Genre: Western / Action | Platform: Theatrical | Director: Quentin Tarantino

Django Unchained is a fascinating case because both its woke and traditional scores are substantial. The woke side comes from the subject matter: slavery, racial revenge, and the systematic evil of the antebellum South. Tarantino is not subtle about any of this, and the film's righteous fury at the institution of slavery is its emotional engine. The traditional side is equally strong: Django's journey is one of individual agency, martial competence, and the protection of a woman he loves. He earns his freedom through skill and will, not through systemic reform. The film believes in vengeance as justice, in competence as virtue, and in love as the highest loyalty, all of which are traditional values dressed in a revenge-fantasy structure. At +8.1, the traditional side barely edges out the woke side, which lands Django in MIXED territory: a woke film about traditional things, or a traditional film about a woke subject, depending on how you watch it.

#8: The Revenant (2015)

Woke Score: 12.4 • Traditional Score: 21.5 • Verdict: MIXED • Margin: +9.1

Genre: Adventure / Drama / Western | Platform: Theatrical | Director: Alejandro G. Inarritu

The Revenant is a survival story told at the scale of a Western epic, and its ideological profile mirrors its physical setting: harsh, beautiful, and ambiguous. The woke score at 12.4 comes from the film's treatment of Native Americans, its criticism of frontier exploitation, and its general suspicion of the civilizing impulse that drove westward expansion. The traditional score at 21.5 comes from Hugh Glass's relentless will to survive, his loyalty to his son, and the film's conviction that endurance is a moral quality. The Revenant does not preach; it shows a man crawling through frozen wilderness for two and a half hours to avenge the murder of his child. That is a traditional story if ever there was one. But the film surrounds that story with enough critique of the frontier project to keep it from crossing fully into traditional-lean territory.

TRADITIONAL LEAN (Margin +10.0 to +19.9)

#10: Unforgiven (1992)

Woke Score: 3.1 • Traditional Score: 17.1 • Verdict: TRADITIONAL LEAN • Margin: +14.0

Genre: Western / Revisionist Western / Drama | Platform: Max / Rent or Buy | Director: Clint Eastwood

Clint Eastwood's Unforgiven is the revisionist Western that revisionist Westerns wish they could be: a film that complicates the myth without disowning it. The woke score is minimal because Eastwood has no interest in ideological critique. What he has is a deep ambivalence about violence and the men who commit it. The traditional score comes from the film's unflinching portrait of consequences, its respect for competence, and its final-act refusal to apologize for what William Munny is. Munny does terrible things, and the film does not pretend otherwise, but it also does not pretend he is a monster. He is a man who knows exactly what he is, and when the time comes, he does what he was always going to do. That is not woke. That is not progressive. That is a Western grown old enough to know the cost of what it celebrates, and it is the reason Unforgiven holds the middle of this list with quiet, unshakable authority.

#12: The Good, the Bad and the Ugly (1966)

Woke Score: 1.4 • Traditional Score: 17.1 • Verdict: TRADITIONAL LEAN • Margin: +15.7

Genre: Western / Epic | Platform: Theatrical / Multiple Streamers | Director: Sergio Leone

Sergio Leone's masterpiece carries a near-zero woke score because the film has no ideology at all. It is a story about three men chasing buried gold across a Civil War landscape, and the Civil War is not a political argument but a backdrop of chaos that the characters navigate with cunning, courage, and greed. The traditional score is driven by the film's relentless focus on individual agency: Blondie, Angel Eyes, and Tuco are defined entirely by their choices, and the film judges them entirely by the results of those choices. There is no system to blame and no institution to reform. There is only the desert, the gold, and the moral arithmetic of three men who understand exactly what they are willing to do to get what they want. That is as pure an expression of traditional storytelling as the Western genre has ever produced.

#19: Breaking Bad (2008)

Woke Score: 4.9 • Traditional Score: 30.8 • Verdict: TRADITIONAL • Margin: +25.9

Genre: Crime Drama, Thriller, Neo-Western | Platform: Netflix, AMC+ | Director: Vince Gilligan (creator)

Breaking Bad is the most traditional Western title in the database, and the complete series lands at +25.9 because the show's moral arc is the opposite of what woke storytelling demands. Walter White starts as a victim of the system, a man failed by healthcare, by the economy, by the institutions that were supposed to protect him. A woke version of this story would vindicate him. Breaking Bad does the opposite. It shows that Walt's choices, his pride, his refusal to accept help, his insistence on being the one in control, are the source of his destruction. The show is a five-season argument for personal responsibility, and its final verdict on Walter White is that he was not a victim. He was a man who made decisions, and those decisions destroyed everything he claimed to be protecting. That is a traditional moral universe: actions have consequences, character is destiny, and no amount of systemic unfairness excuses what a man chooses to become. The neo-Western setting, the desert, the frontier of law and crime, is not decoration. It is the moral landscape of the show, and the show uses it to tell the most traditional story in the Western canon: the story of a man who went too far and paid for it.
