A24 is the most acclaimed indie studio in Hollywood. Critics love it. Awards voters love it. The studio has collected more Oscar nominations per film than any distributor of its size. It also has one of the most consistent ideological profiles in the business. That consistency is what this list is about.

VirtueVigil has now reviewed 21 A24 films using our Woke-Watch Scoring System. The data tells a clear story. A24 is not a monolith. The studio produces everything from warrior-class war films with near-zero woke content to prestige dramas with explicit progressive framing. The ideological range is enormous: from Euphoria at -38 WOKE to Exit 8 at +31 TRAD. That is a 69-point spread within a single studio. The average leans left, and the most celebrated releases, the ones that win Oscars and get written about in major publications, tend to cluster in the woke or woke-lean categories. But the most traditional films in the A24 catalog are also its newest. The 2026 releases include the three most conservative A24 films ever made.

Below, every A24 film in our database is ranked from most woke to least woke. The ranking is based on score margin: the difference between woke score and traditional score. Films with a negative margin lean woke. Films with a positive margin lean traditional. Each entry draws directly from our full review data. No fabricated scores, no guesswork.

  • 1

    Euphoria - Season 1 (2019)

    STRONGLY WOKE -38 WOKE

    Sam Levinson's HBO series is the most ideologically aggressive title in the A24 catalog by a wide margin. The woke score of 43.5 against a trad score of 5.3 produces a -38 margin that is not close. The show's ideology is embedded in its assumptions: that gender is performed, that sexuality is the primary site of identity, that traditional institutions cannot help you, and that beauty and trauma are inseparable. Jules Vaughn, a trans girl played by Hunter Schafer, is the show's best person and its most effective ideological argument. The show is also, paradoxically, one of the most effective anti-drug, anti-promiscuity arguments ever aired on television. Every character who uses drugs suffers. Every character who treats sex as recreation is harmed. Euphoria is a text at war with itself, and the war is worth studying.

    Midsommar (2019)

    WOKE -18 WOKE

    Ari Aster's sunlit horror film is technically extraordinary and ideologically transparent. The woke score of 20.58 against a trad score of 2.4 makes it the second most woke A24 film in our database. The film's ideological project is the presentation of feminist liberation through the lens of ritual murder. The male characters exist on a spectrum from useless to monstrous, and Dani's eventual choice to burn her boyfriend alive is framed as catharsis. The progressive critical establishment celebrated the ending as a breakup movie. The craft is extraordinary. What the craft is serving is not.

    Warfare (2025)

    TRADITIONAL LEAN +7 TRAD

    Exit 8 (2026)

    STRONGLY TRADITIONAL +31 TRAD

    Genki Kawamura's adaptation of the viral indie horror game is the most traditional film in A24's entire catalog. The woke score of 0.7 and trad score of 31.64 produce a +31 TRAD margin. The premise is a looping subway corridor, but the question is not "can you spot the anomaly?" It is "are you ready to be a father?" Kazunari Ninomiya carries the film as a man whose stillness is either strength or paralysis, and the entire 95-minute engine is dedicated to collapsing that ambiguity. The pregnancy is never framed as a political issue. It is framed as a test. Fatherhood is the highest stake. Masculine accountability is the engine. Redemption through earned action is the resolution. A 97% Rotten Tomatoes score and a Cannes standing ovation. This combination is rare. Do not miss it.

What the Data Says About A24

Twenty-one films reviewed. Eleven lean traditional or stronger. Nine lean woke or stronger. One is a perfect dead heat. That is not the studio profile that A24's reputation suggests, but the picture is more complicated than the headline number. The most celebrated A24 releases, the ones that win Oscars and get written about in major publications, tend to cluster in the woke or woke-lean categories. Euphoria, Midsommar, Babygirl, Eddington, The Brutalist, Everything Everywhere All at Once: these are the films that define A24's cultural identity. Exit 8, The Death of Robin Hood, Backrooms, and Warfare exist in the same catalog, but they are not the films the studio markets as its identity.

The most interesting finding is temporal. The three most traditional A24 films in our database, Exit 8, The Death of Robin Hood, and Backrooms, are all 2026 releases. The 2026 A24 slate produced the studio's most conservative films ever, alongside some of its most progressive (The Drama, The Invite). The ideological range is not narrowing. It is widening. A24 is becoming harder to pin down, not easier.

The honest read: A24 is a studio with genuine range and a genuine ideological tilt. It will greenlight a zero-woke war film and a lesbian coming-of-age film and a submission-fantasy Nicole Kidman drama in the same year without blinking. The throughline is not ideology. It is craft and controversy. Whatever gets talked about, A24 will make it. And right now, what gets talked about skews left, but the data is starting to tell a more complicated story.
