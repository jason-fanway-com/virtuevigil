Quentin Tarantino has made exactly nine films if you count Kill Bill as one, or ten if you split it. He says he will stop at ten. Every one of his films is a conversation with cinema history -- with the grindhouse, the spaghetti western, the blaxploitation picture, the French New Wave. But they are also conversations with the culture, and like any three-decade conversation with the culture, they reveal something about the person having it.

Tarantino is a singular filmmaker in this regard. He came out of the gate in 1992 with Reservoir Dogs, a film that treats masculinity as performance and violence as farce, with traditional values barely registering. By 2019, he made Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, a film about a man protecting his home, his friend, and an innocent woman from the Manson Family -- and scored his most traditional film by a wide margin. In between, the arc is not straight. It zigs and zags. But the overall direction is unmistakable.

VirtueVigil has reviewed and scored every Tarantino feature film using the same dual-metric VVWS methodology we apply to every review. Traditional Score measures the presence of traditional values: courage, sacrifice, honor, family, loyalty, faith, duty. Woke Score measures progressive ideological content: identity politics, institutional critique, gender theory, intersectional framing, anti-traditional messaging. Margin is the gap between them. A positive margin means the progressive content outweighs the traditional. A negative margin means traditional values carry the film. Rankings run from most woke (top) to most traditional (bottom). Every entry links to its full VirtueVigil review with trope-by-trope breakdown. We reviewed the six Tarantino films in our database: Reservoir Dogs, Pulp Fiction, Kill Bill: Volume 1, Inglourious Basterds, Django Unchained, and Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. Jackie Brown, Death Proof, and The Hateful Eight are not yet in the catalog.

WOKE LEAN

Two films cross into progressive territory, both from Tarantino's early career. Reservoir Dogs and Pulp Fiction share a common thread: traditional institutions are either absent, hollow, or the punchline.

#1 (Most Woke): Pulp Fiction (1994)

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 18.0 TRAD: 10.1 MARGIN: +7.9 WOKE

Genre: Crime / Drama • Year: 1994 • Runtime: 154 min • Rating: R • Platform: Theatrical

Pulp Fiction is the most woke film Tarantino has made, and that statement deserves explanation because Pulp Fiction does not read as a progressive film. It reads as a postmodern crime collage. But that is exactly the point. Pulp Fiction's 18.0 woke score is driven not by overt political messaging but by the systematic absence of traditional moral architecture. The film's world is one where honor is a joke (the Wolf cleans up corpses, not souls), where faith is a survival mechanism rather than a conviction (Jules's recitation of Ezekiel is a hitman's ritual, and his decision to walk the earth is a dodge, not a conversion), and where romantic love takes the form of a hitman dancing with his boss's wife before she nearly dies of an overdose. The 10.1 traditional score is the lowest in Tarantino's catalog. The film offers no positive vision of how people should live. What it offers, brilliantly, is a world where everyone is scrambling, and the scramble is the entire show. The iconic status of Pulp Fiction as the defining film of the 1990s independent cinema movement should not obscure what it actually believes about human nature. It believes people are fundamentally transactional, that institutions are fronts, and that the only moral difference between characters is whether they are lucky enough to walk away. That is a progressive worldview dressed in a sharkskin suit.

#2: Reservoir Dogs (1992)

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 20.4 TRAD: 16.4 MARGIN: +4.0 WOKE

Genre: Crime / Thriller • Year: 1992 • Runtime: 99 min • Rating: R • Platform: Theatrical

Reservoir Dogs arrived in 1992 like a pipe bomb in a tuxedo. Tarantino's debut has the highest woke score of any film in his catalog at 20.4, but it is also the closest call, with a 16.4 traditional score that keeps the film in WOKE LEAN rather than outright WOKE. The woke score is driven by the same mechanism as Pulp Fiction: traditional institutions are absent or perverse. Mr. Blonde is a psychopath whose loyalty to the crew is performative. Mr. Orange's undercover identity collapses the distinction between cop and criminal until neither category means anything. Mr. White's code of loyalty -- the one genuinely traditional element in the film -- gets him killed. The traditional score comes from that element: White's insistence on honor among thieves is treated by the film as admirable, even noble, even though it destroys him. The film does not mock White's code. It mourns it. That is the difference between Reservoir Dogs and the nihilism of a pure woke text. Tarantino's debut is a film about what happens when men without moral foundations try to operate in a world that demands them. The answer is a warehouse full of blood and one man bleeding out while the radio plays.

TRADITIONAL LEAN

Three films fall into the TRADITIONAL LEAN verdict band. Two are revenge stories. One is a war film. All three feature protagonists who believe in something, and that belief is the engine of the story, not the butt of the joke.

#3: Kill Bill: Volume 1 (2003)

TRADITIONAL LEAN TRAD: 18.5 WOKE: 13.4 MARGIN: +5.1 TRAD

Genre: Action / Martial Arts / Revenge • Year: 2003 • Runtime: 111 min • Rating: R • Platform: Theatrical

Kill Bill: Volume 1 is where Tarantino's ideological arc begins to bend toward the traditional, and the mechanism is simple: motherhood. The Bride's entire campaign of vengeance is driven by the murder of her unborn child and the massacre of her wedding party. That is not a progressive motivation. It is the most ancient and conservative motivation in storytelling: a mother avenging her child and reclaiming her identity. The 18.5 traditional score is driven by that core. The Bride's quest is righteous, not ambivalent. Her enemies are genuinely evil. The film does not ask you to sympathize with O-Ren Ishii's tragic backstory or to question whether the Bride is perpetuating a cycle of violence. It gives you a woman in a hospital bed, waking up to discover her baby is gone, and then it gives you a Hattori Hanzo sword. The 13.4 woke score comes from the film's stylistic DNA -- the blaxploitation and kung-fu references are rooted in progressive cultural aesthetics, and the female-fronted violence narrative carries progressive framing even when the underlying motivation is traditional. But the film's moral gravity is unmistakably conservative. Revenge as justice is older than democracy.

#4: Inglourious Basterds (2009)

TRADITIONAL LEAN TRAD: 16.5 WOKE: 9.4 MARGIN: +7.2 TRAD

Genre: War / Drama / Black Comedy • Year: 2009 • Runtime: 153 min • Rating: R • Platform: Theatrical

Inglourious Basterds is a Jewish revenge fantasy set during World War II, and that description alone raises the question: how does a film about killing Nazis score TRADITIONAL LEAN? The answer is in what the film treats as sacred. The Basterds do not kill Nazis because Nazis are politically inconvenient. They kill Nazis because Nazis are evil, and the film never hedges on that point. Hans Landa is the most charming character in the film and also the most despicable, and the film's thesis is that charm is a tool of evil, not its mitigation. The 16.5 traditional score comes from the unapologetic moral clarity: the good guys scalp Nazis, the bad guys are Nazis, and the only moral question is how thoroughly the Nazis should be destroyed. The 9.4 woke score reflects Tarantino's use of historical revisionism (the theater massacre rewrites the actual end of the Nazi high command, which is a progressive technique of reclaiming history for ideological purposes) and the film's self-conscious commentary on cinema as propaganda. But the film's moral engine is conservative in the deepest sense: it believes evil exists, it believes evil should be confronted with maximum force, and it refuses to equivocate about which side is which. Shoshanna's face projected onto the smoke-filled screen, announcing to the Nazi high command that they are about to be killed by a Jew, is one of the most cathartic moments in cinema. It earns that catharsis because the film never asks you to feel bad about it.

#5: Django Unchained (2012)

TRADITIONAL LEAN TRAD: 21.5 WOKE: 13.4 MARGIN: +8.1 TRAD

Genre: Western / Action • Year: 2012 • Runtime: 165 min • Rating: R • Platform: Theatrical

Django Unchained is Tarantino's most ideologically complex film, with the highest traditional score (21.5) of his TRADITIONAL LEAN entries and a substantial 13.4 woke score reflecting the film's engagement with American racial history. The traditional score comes from the same place as Kill Bill and Basterds: the revenge narrative, executed without irony. Django's quest to rescue his wife Broomhilda from Calvin Candie's plantation is a marriage-rescue story, one of the oldest traditional arcs in narrative history. Django is not seeking social justice or systemic reform. He is seeking his wife. The institution of marriage is the moral anchor that justifies every act of violence in the film. The woke score reflects the film's setting and subject matter: slavery is an inherently progressive topic in modern American cinema, and Tarantino's approach -- using the spaghetti western, a genre built on morally ambiguous antiheroes, to tell a story where the moral lines are absolutely clear -- forces the audience to sit with the reality of American slavery in a way that prestige dramas often soften. The film's treatment of violence against slaveholders is intentionally uncomfortable and deliberately cathartic: Tarantino wants you to cheer when Django kills white slavers, and he wants you to notice that you are cheering. That reflexive awareness is a progressive technique even when deployed in service of a traditional narrative.

TRADITIONAL

One Tarantino film crosses into outright TRADITIONAL territory, and it is the one that is most explicitly about the thing Tarantino's entire career has been a love letter to: the movies themselves, and the people who make them.

#6 (Most Traditional): Once Upon a Time in Hollywood (2019)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 19.2 WOKE: 6.8 MARGIN: +12.4 TRAD

Genre: Drama / Comedy • Year: 2019 • Runtime: 161 min • Rating: R • Platform: Theatrical

Once Upon a Time in Hollywood is the most traditional film Quentin Tarantino has ever made, and it is not close. The 6.8 woke score is his lowest. The +12.4 traditional margin is his strongest. The film is a love letter to a version of Los Angeles that existed for about six months in 1969, and the love is not ironic. Rick Dalton is a fading television star struggling with irrelevance and alcoholism. He is not a hero. He is a man trying to stay employed, stay sober, and stay friends with the only person who still believes in him: his stunt double, Cliff Booth. That friendship is the film's moral center: loyalty without expectation of reward, the kind of bond that produces genuine self-sacrifice. The woke score is low because the film refuses to judge its characters by contemporary standards. It does not apologize for Cliff Booth's past. It does not lecture Rick Dalton about toxic masculinity. It simply observes them -- two men at the end of their era, facing obsolescence with dignity. The film's revisionist climax, in which the Manson Family murders are redirected away from Sharon Tate and toward Rick Dalton's house, is Tarantino's most conservative creative decision: he rewrites history to protect an innocent woman and her unborn child, and he gives the job of protection to two men who, by every measure of 2019 cultural politics, should not be the heroes of anything. The flamethrower is deployed against progressive nihilism itself. It is the most satisfying ending in Tarantino's filmography, and it proves something his early work deliberately obscured: Quentin Tarantino, at the end of his career, believes in good and evil, and he believes good should win.

The Arc of a Career

Six films. Three decades. Two WOKE LEAN. Three TRADITIONAL LEAN. One TRADITIONAL. The arc is visible and, in retrospect, unsurprising.

Tarantino began his career as a video store clerk who absorbed a generation of progressive film theory and post-structuralist technique. Reservoir Dogs and Pulp Fiction are products of that formation: formally radical, morally ambivalent, institutionally skeptical. They treat traditional values as either absent or decorative. Characters have codes, but those codes are personal aesthetics, not moral commitments. Nothing holds. Everything is performance.

Something changed in the 2000s. Kill Bill and Inglourious Basterds are still formally playful, but their moral engines are different. The Bride's quest is righteous. Aldo Raine's war is just. The films do not deconstruct the revenge narrative. They inhabit it. They believe in it. The shift from WOKE LEAN to TRADITIONAL LEAN is a shift from Tarantino the postmodernist to Tarantino the moralist, and it is a shift that mirrors a broader cultural exhaustion with irony.

Django Unchained pushes this further, using the most politically charged subject in American history to tell a story about a man rescuing his wife. The traditional values are the spine; the progressive context is the setting. The film's identity politics are present, but they are subordinated to the marriage-rescue narrative, not the other way around. That is the difference between Django Unchained and a film like 12 Years a Slave: Tarantino's moral interest is in the individual, not the system.

Once Upon a Time in Hollywood completes the arc. It is a film about protecting home, defending the innocent, and honoring friendship. The evil is real -- the Manson Family is not a metaphor for anything; it is literal evil -- and the good is real, embodied by two men who are past their prime, past their relevance, and still show up. Tarantino waited until his ninth film to reveal what he actually believes. It turns out he believes in the same things John Ford believed. He just took the long way home.

The three missing films -- Jackie Brown, Death Proof, and The Hateful Eight -- will eventually be added to the VirtueVigil catalog. Jackie Brown, Tarantino's sole adaptation, is the film most likely to shift the ranking: its middle-aged female protagonist and blaxploitation homage could land anywhere from MIXED to TRADITIONAL LEAN. Death Proof, as a grindhouse exercise, will likely score similarly to Reservoir Dogs. The Hateful Eight, a post-Civil War chamber piece about trust and betrayal, should fit comfortably alongside Django and Basterds in TRADITIONAL LEAN territory. But the overall shape of the arc will hold: from ironic to sincere, from deconstruction to conviction, from the video store to the hearth.
