2024 was the year the culture cracked open.

It was the year Disney spent $230 million on a Star Wars series that audiences rejected so hard it was cancelled after eight episodes. It was the year the Academy gave Best Picture to a film about a sex worker whose director thanked sex workers from the Oscar stage. It was the year a transgender cartel musical earned 13 Oscar nominations and managed to offend progressives and conservatives simultaneously. It was the year Wicked sold itself as a family-friendly Thanksgiving musical while delivering two hours and forty minutes of progressive allegory to church groups who had no idea what they were buying tickets for.

Our database tracked 112 films and shows released in 2024. More than half carry a BALANCED WOKE verdict or stronger. Nine land in WOKE territory. One is STRONGLY WOKE. The ideological content in last year's most celebrated films was not subtle, not hidden in subtext, and not accidental. It was the product. The prestige film pipeline and the progressive messaging pipeline converged completely in 2024, and the awards circuit rewarded almost all of it.

This is not a list of bad films. Several films on this list are genuinely excellent -- well-acted, beautifully shot, masterfully written. That is what makes them worth understanding. A clumsy propaganda film bounces off. A great film with a progressive argument seeps in. You do not notice it happening. By the time the credits roll, you have absorbed a worldview without ever deciding to.

The 20 films and shows below are ranked from most woke to least woke, using the same VirtueVigil Wake Score methodology we apply to every review in our database. Each entry includes woke and traditional scores, the verdict, what parents and viewers need to know, and a link to the full review for readers who want the complete trope breakdown. This is not about banning art. It is about knowing what you are watching -- and deciding, with full information, what belongs in your home.

WOKE (Margin -10.0 to -38.6)

#2: Conclave (2024)

Woke Score: 42.6 • Traditional Score: 3.9 • Verdict: WOKE • Margin: -38.7 WOKE

Genre: Thriller | Platform: Streaming

Edward Berger's papal election thriller is the most dangerous kind of progressive film: one that is genuinely excellent. Ralph Fiennes delivers a career performance. The cinematography is austere and magnificent. The ritual and ceremony of the papal election is rendered with reverential care that makes you believe the filmmakers respect what they are filming. They do not. The plot reveals that the progressive candidate was planted in the Vatican by a previous liberal pope, and the film treats this as a heroic twist rather than what it actually is: institutional subversion dressed as divine intervention. The conservative candidate is framed as a bigot whose bigotry is the only thing disqualifying him. The moderate candidate is weak. Only the progressive -- the one installed by stealth -- is worthy of the papacy. At 42.6 woke to 3.9 traditional, the gap is a chasm. The craft makes the argument persuasive. That is the trap.

#3: Heretic (2024)

Woke Score: 41.2 • Traditional Score: 13.7 • Verdict: WOKE • Margin: -27.5 WOKE

Genre: Horror | Platform: Streaming

Hugh Grant gives his most charming, murderous performance as a man who traps two Mormon missionaries in his house and spends the next two hours dismantling their faith. Heretic is not a horror film that happens to touch on religion. It is a theological assault on Christianity -- and on all monotheistic faith -- dressed in genre clothing. The two missionaries are not complex characters. They are props, foils for the film's real hero: a sadistic atheist who argues that religion is a con, an evolutionary iteration of ancient control systems. The film stacks the deck completely. Grant's character is witty, intelligent, and prepared. The missionaries are naive, sheltered, and wrong. At 41.2 woke to 13.7 traditional, the 13.7 traditional score reflects some genuinely thoughtful moments -- but those moments exist only to be demolished. For parents with teens who may encounter this through streaming, the horror elements are the least dangerous thing in the film.

#6: The Substance (2024)

Woke Score: 37.3 • Traditional Score: 13.2 • Verdict: WOKE • Margin: -24.1 WOKE

Genre: Horror | Platform: Streaming

Coralie Fargeat used 21,000 liters of fake blood to make a point about how Hollywood discards women over 40. The Substance follows Elisabeth Sparkle (Demi Moore), an aging actress who injects a mysterious compound that births a younger version of herself (Margaret Qualley) -- with the rule that they must trade consciousness every seven days. The body horror is extreme, the satire is unsubtle, and the film's feminist argument about beauty standards and the male gaze operates at full volume from the first frame to the last. At 37.3 woke to 13.2 traditional, it is one of the year's most ideologically coherent films, and its horror-genre wrapper makes its arguments feel like spectacle rather than sermon -- which is exactly why they land.

#7: Immaculate (2024)

Woke Score: 25.4 • Traditional Score: 3.5 • Verdict: WOKE • Margin: -21.9 WOKE

Genre: Horror | Platform: Theatrical / Hulu

Sydney Sweeney spent years developing Immaculate as a vehicle to carry a message she clearly believed in. The film -- structured as Rosemary's Baby filtered through contemporary reproductive politics -- follows a young American nun who discovers she is pregnant despite being a virgin. The convent treats this as a miracle. The film treats it as a prison. The third act reveals that the pregnancy was orchestrated by the Church to breed a new savior, and the final shot is Sweeney's character smashing a rock onto the newborn's head while screaming. For devout Catholic viewers, Immaculate is one of the most precisely targeted woke traps of the past several years. The horror framing masks the ideological payload. The Catholic iconography gives the film a religious audience that a secular version of the same story would never attract. At 25.4 woke to 3.5 traditional, the gap is deliberate. Immaculate knows exactly who it is talking to -- and what it wants them to feel by the end.

#8: True Detective: Night Country (2024)

Nineteen Emmy nominations. Jodie Foster's first major television role. The highest viewership in the True Detective anthology's history. Issa Lopez replaced the philosophical brooding of the first three seasons with a checklist of 2024 identity politics: indigenous women murdered at epidemic rates, white male scientists as literal villains, the mining company as extractive colonial evil, the supernatural mythology reframed as Indigenous justice. #10: Blitz (2024)

Woke Score: 33.0 • Traditional Score: 14.7 • Verdict: WOKE LEAN • Margin: -18.3 WOKE

Genre: Drama | Platform: Apple TV+

Steve McQueen's World War II drama arrives dressed in the clothing of a wartime survival story, the kind of tale that should celebrate British resilience, familial devotion, and the quiet heroism of ordinary people. Those elements genuinely exist here. Saoirse Ronan delivers a deeply committed performance as a mother searching for her evacuated son. Young Elliott Heffernan carries real emotional weight. But the film cannot help inserting its politics: the Blitz is rendered as a multicultural morality play where the real threat is not German bombs but British racism. A Nigerian air raid warden lectures white Londoners about prejudice while saving their lives. The point is not wrong -- racism existed in wartime Britain -- but the film's instinct to foreground it at the expense of the actual war flattens the story into allegory. At 33.0 woke to 14.7 traditional, Blitz has too much craft to dismiss. It also has too much ideology to trust.

Diablo Cody's Jennifer's Body was dismissed on release and later reclaimed as a feminist cult classic. Lisa Frankenstein follows the same template: misfit female protagonist, supernatural acquisition of power, male targets consumed or dispatched, cathartic empowerment as the film's central thesis. Set in 1989 and styled like a John Hughes film if John Hughes had been possessed by a demon, the story follows Lisa, an unpopular teenager who accidentally reanimates a Victorian corpse and discovers that killing people together is a great way to bond. Woke Score: 21.0 • Traditional Score: 6.6 • Verdict: BALANCED WOKE • Margin: -14.4 WOKE

Genre: Biographical Drama | Platform: Theatrical (Briarcliff Entertainment)

There is something fascinating about The Apprentice, and it is not what its makers intended. Ali Abbasi and Gabriel Sherman set out to make a Trump origin story that explains the 45th and 47th president as a creation of Roy Cohn, the ruthless attorney who once served as Joseph McCarthy's attack dog. The thesis is that everything Trump became -- the relentless aggression, the refusal to admit weakness, the attack-attack-attack posture -- was downloaded from Cohn in 1970s and 1980s Manhattan. But Sebastian Stan's performance is so committed, and the film's portrait is so detailed, that it accidentally humanizes Trump in ways the filmmakers did not intend. At 21.0 woke to 6.6 traditional, the BALANCED WOKE verdict reflects a film whose ideological intentions are clear but whose execution undermines them. For anyone on either side of the aisle, it is a more interesting film than the marketing suggests.

#18: Nickel Boys (2024)

Woke Score: 21.8 • Traditional Score: 8.7 • Verdict: BALANCED WOKE • Margin: -13.1 WOKE

Genre: Drama | Platform: Theatrical (Orion Pictures / Amazon MGM Studios) | Director: RaMell Ross | Rating: PG-13

RaMell Ross has made a film that does not want you to watch it -- it wants you to live inside it. Nickel Boys adapts Colson Whitehead's Pulitzer Prize-winning novel about Elwood Curtis, an idealistic Black teenager in 1960s Tallahassee who is wrongfully sent to Nickel Academy, a fictional reform school based on the real Dozier School for Boys where more than 50 boys were buried in unmarked graves. The film is shot entirely in first-person perspective -- the camera is the character's eyes. The effect is immersive and sometimes overwhelming. At 21.8 woke to 8.7 traditional, BALANCED WOKE is the correct verdict: the film's subject matter is inherently political, but Ross's visual approach treats it as a human story rather than a polemic. The PG-13 rating means this could be shown in schools. Parents should know: the film is honest about injustice without being manipulative about it. That honesty does not mean the worldview is neutral. It means the craft earns the argument it makes.

#19: Megalopolis (2024)

Woke Score: 20.8 • Traditional Score: 9.0 • Verdict: BALANCED WOKE • Margin: -11.8 WOKE

Genre: Epic / Science Fiction / Political Drama | Platform: Theatrical / VOD

Francis Ford Coppola spent 40 years and $120 million of his own money making a film that argues, without ambiguity or subtlety, that the future belongs to progressive utopianism. Adam Driver plays Cesar Catalina, an architect who can stop time and dreams of building Megalopolis -- a utopian city where everyone is an artist, no one works, and the old order of politics and power dissolves into creative anarchy. The production design is extraordinary. The ambition is staggering. The coherence is... not. At 20.8 woke to 9.0 traditional, Megalopolis is not a dangerous film. The culture has already forgotten it. But as an artifact of 2024's ideological moment -- a Cannes-premiered epic that argues the future is progressive by definition -- it belongs on this list.

#20: Wicked (2024)

Woke Score: 28.4 • Traditional Score: 17.4 • Verdict: BALANCED WOKE • Margin: -11.0 WOKE

Genre: Musical | Platform: Theatrical / Peacock

Somewhere between the 400 brand partnerships, the pink-and-green Stanley cups, and the church-group holiday matinees, something got lost in translation. Universal sold Wicked as the feel-good movie event of 2024. A splashy, joyful, family-safe musical spectacle perfect for Thanksgiving weekend. What the marketing did not mention is that this particular story has functioned as a progressive anthem for two decades: the green-skinned witch is not evil, she is misunderstood -- a victim of a smear campaign by a corrupt authoritarian who uses propaganda to turn the public against her. The allegory could not be clearer in 2024. Cynthia Erivo and Ariana Grande are both magnificent. The production values are staggering. But at 28.4 woke to 17.4 traditional, Wicked earns its BALANCED WOKE verdict honestly -- the traditional score is the highest on this list, reflecting genuine moments of friendship, sacrifice, and moral courage that transcend the allegory. For parents who took their kids to see it thinking it was just a fun musical: the songs are great. The message is not neutral. Both things are true. And Part 2 arrives in 2025.
