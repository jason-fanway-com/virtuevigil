Hollywood has always loved a true story. The problem is what happens to the truth between the screenplay and the screen. Biopics and historical films claim to show us real people and real events, but every frame is a choice: what to include, what to omit, whose perspective matters, and what lesson the audience should take home.

VirtueVigil watched 63 of them. We scored each one using the same dual-axis methodology we apply to every film: tracking progressive ideology on one side and traditional values on the other. The margin between those scores tells you whether the film told its true story straight or bent the truth to fit a political narrative.

What we found: Hollywood's biographical impulse leans traditional overall, but the outliers at both ends tell a story of their own. The most woke biopics actively reshape history to serve contemporary progressive narratives. The most traditional ones let their subjects speak for themselves, flaws and all. Rankings run from most woke (top) to most traditional (bottom). Every entry links to its full VirtueVigil review with trope-by-trope breakdown, parental guidance, and scoring detail.

The Pattern

Look at the distribution. Two WOKE entries at the top. Twenty-three STRONGLY TRADITIONAL entries at the bottom. The center of gravity is unmistakable: when Hollywood tells true stories, the material itself pushes back against ideological overlay. Real lives resist reduction to political morality plays.

The woke biopics on this list share a common strategy: select a subject, then reframe that subject's life as evidence in a predetermined case against patriarchy, capitalism, or American institutions. The traditional biopics do the opposite: they let the subject's own beliefs, achievements, and struggles shape the narrative, even when those beliefs are unfashionable.

The film with the highest traditional margin -- Risen at -50.4 -- is about a man rising from the dead. The film with the highest woke margin -- The Apprentice at +14.4 -- is about a man who became president. Both are arguments. Only one admits it.
