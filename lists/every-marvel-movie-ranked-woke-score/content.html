The Marvel Cinematic Universe is the most successful franchise in film history. Twenty-one films reviewed. $30 billion in global box office. A generation of viewers who grew up with Tony Stark, Steve Rogers, and Peter Parker as household names. For two decades, Marvel has been the central engine of American popular entertainment.

It has also been an ideological battleground, fought with increasing intensity as the franchise has aged. The MCU's early films were built on straightforward heroic archetypes: sacrifice, duty, earned power, protection of the innocent. Its later output introduced progressive politics with varying degrees of subtlety, from the all-female splash shot in Endgame to Captain Marvel's explicit positioning as a feminist statement. The question of where each film falls on the ideological spectrum has become increasingly relevant for parents deciding what to watch with their families.

VirtueVigil has now reviewed every MCU film using our dual-scoring system, which measures the density and intensity of both traditional values content and progressive ideological content independently. These are not quality ratings. Some of the most traditional films on this list are flawed. Some of the most woke are technically accomplished. The score reflects ideology, not craft.

The list runs from most traditional at the top to most woke at the bottom. Every film links to its full VirtueVigil review with complete category breakdowns. We are presenting the entire MCU in order, from the films that affirm the values the franchise was built on to the ones that abandoned them.

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL

#1. Captain America: The First Avenger (2011)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 26.9 WOKE: 3.8 MARGIN: +23.1 TRAD

Director: Joe Johnston • Stars: Chris Evans, Hayley Atwell, Hugo Weaving • Year: 2011

Fifteen years of Marvel content later, nothing has touched Captain America: The First Avenger for pure unironic patriotism. Joe Johnston directed it as a genuine World War II adventure rather than a deconstruction of one, and the difference is visible in every frame. Steve Rogers is a man of character before he is a man of power. His transformation into Captain America affirms that virtue precedes strength: the super-soldier serum amplifies what is already inside the man. The film treats self-sacrifice, national loyalty, and duty without irony or qualification. Chris Evans plays Steve straight, no wink, no postmodern distance, and the result is the highest traditional score in the entire MCU. The 3.8 woke score reflects Peggy Carter's capable wartime role, which is handled with period-appropriate restraint rather than contemporary revisionism.

TRADITIONAL

#2. Spider-Man: No Way Home (2021)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 22.1 WOKE: 3.5 MARGIN: +18.6 TRAD

Director: Jon Watts • Stars: Tom Holland, Tobey Maguire, Andrew Garfield • Year: 2021

Jon Watts and writers McKenna and Sommers faced an impossible task: take a film already crammed with five returning villains from two previous Spider-Man film universes and somehow make it emotionally coherent. No Way Home is not just the best Spider-Man film ever made. It is one of the most emotionally devastating blockbusters of the decade, and it earned every tear. Peter Parker makes the morally traditional choice to try to save his villains rather than send them home to die, and the film treats that decision with complete seriousness even as it costs him everything. Aunt May's death and the spell of forgetting at the end are not plot mechanics. They are consequences of a young man choosing to do the right thing, and the film never undercuts their weight. The 3.5 woke score is one of the lowest in the MCU.

#3. Logan (2017)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 23.5 WOKE: 5.8 MARGIN: +17.7 TRAD

Director: James Mangold • Stars: Hugh Jackman, Patrick Stewart, Dafne Keen • Year: 2017

There is a scene near the end of Logan where Laura cradles the dying Wolverine, turns the cross marking his grave sideways to make an X, and cries for the first time in the film. If that moment does not hit you somewhere real, I am not sure what would. James Mangold's Logan is the best superhero film of the modern era, and its moral architecture is deeply conservative. A broken father figure protects a child who represents the future. The cost of that protection is everything: his health, his peace, his friends, his life. Hugh Jackman plays Wolverine as a man who has outlived everyone he loved and is done with the world, only to discover that he is not done protecting what can still be saved. The film earns a 5.8 woke score mostly from corporate villainy and the weaponization of children, themes that are used in service of a traditionally structured redemption narrative rather than as ideological ends in themselves.

#4. Ant-Man (2015)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 21.0 WOKE: 3.9 MARGIN: +17.1 TRAD

Director: Peyton Reed • Stars: Paul Rudd, Evangeline Lilly, Michael Douglas • Year: 2015

Ant-Man is the MCU's stealth traditionalist. It looks like a heist comedy. It is actually a father-daughter story wrapped in a redemption arc. Scott Lang is an ex-con who cannot hold a job and cannot see his daughter, and what drives his entire arc is the desire to be worthy of her. Hank Pym's relationship with Hope mirrors the same dynamic from the other direction: a father who lost his wife and pushed his daughter away, trying to repair the damage before it is too late. The villain is a corporate predator trying to weaponize Pym's technology for profit and military escalation, which the film treats as a straightforward moral wrong. No ideological flags. No political subtext. Just a man trying to be the father his daughter deserves, at +17.1 traditional margin.

#5. The Fantastic Four: First Steps (2026)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 19.0 WOKE: 3.1 MARGIN: +15.9 TRAD

Director: Matt Shakman • Stars: Pedro Pascal, Vanessa Kirby, Joseph Quinn, Ebon Moss-Bachrach • Year: 2026

Two Fox attempts that ranged from disappointing to catastrophic left a generation of comics readers skeptical that Marvel's First Family could ever work on screen. Matt Shakman's First Steps proves them wrong. The Fantastic Four's unique superpower as a team has always been that they are a family first and superheroes second. Reed Richards and Sue Storm are married. Johnny is Sue's brother. Ben Grimm is the family's loyal friend who paid the highest physical price. The film treats those relationships as the foundation, not the decoration. The retro-futuristic 1960s aesthetic serves a thematic purpose: this is Marvel before irony, before deconstruction, when heroism was clean. At +15.9 traditional and just 3.1 woke, First Steps is the most traditionally scored MCU film of 2026 and one of the cleanest in the entire franchise.

#6. Avengers: Infinity War (2018)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 16.8 WOKE: 2.0 MARGIN: +14.8 TRAD

Directors: Anthony Russo, Joe Russo • Stars: Robert Downey Jr., Josh Brolin, Chris Hemsworth • Year: 2018

Avengers: Infinity War is the best argument the Marvel Cinematic Universe ever made for its own existence. Ten years of narrative groundwork paid off in a film that is, almost in spite of itself, genuinely tragic. The heroes lose. The villain wins. The final fifteen minutes are among the most audacious in blockbuster history. Thanos is the rare MCU antagonist who operates with a coherent moral framework, however monstrous: he believes his purpose is to save the universe from itself, and the film gives him the conviction and screen time to make that belief feel earned rather than recited. Against him, the heroes' sacrifices take on genuine weight. Gamora dies. Vision dies. Loki dies. Half the universe dissolves. The film's 2.0 woke score is among the lowest in the franchise because the Russos were not interested in ideology. They were interested in stakes, and stakes are what they delivered.

#7. Spider-Man: Web of Heroes (2026)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 21.4 WOKE: 6.8 MARGIN: +14.6 TRAD

Director: Jon Watts • Stars: Tom Holland, Zendaya, Jacob Batalon • Year: 2026

Tom Holland's Spider-Man films have maintained something crucial that much contemporary superhero cinema has lost: a genuine moral framework. Peter Parker is not a tortured antihero questioning whether he should use his power. He is a young person learning that he must use his power, and that using it will cost him something real. Web of Heroes continues that tradition. The multiverse concept, which could easily have become a gimmick, is instead deployed to explore what makes Peter Parker Peter Parker across every version of the character. The answer the film settles on is responsibility: the burden that defines him, the moral core that separates him from every other Spider-variant. The 6.8 woke score comes from the ensemble's diversity and a few contemporary character beats, none of which undermine the film's traditional center.

#8. Captain America: The Winter Soldier (2014)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 25.6 WOKE: 11.0 MARGIN: +14.6 TRAD

Directors: Anthony Russo, Joe Russo • Stars: Chris Evans, Scarlett Johansson, Sebastian Stan • Year: 2014

Ten years into the MCU's run, The Winter Soldier still holds up as the franchise's best individual film. It knows what it is: a 1970s political thriller wearing a superhero costume, and it is completely serious about both halves of that description. Steve Rogers discovers that SHIELD, the institution he trusted, has been infiltrated by Hydra and is about to launch a global surveillance operation that would eliminate individual liberty at scale. His response is not moral compromise. It is moral clarity: burn it all down. The film's skepticism of institutional power and mass surveillance lands across the political spectrum, but its governing ethic is conservative at the root. Rogers stands for individual freedom against collectivist control, and he stands alone when he has to. The 11.0 woke score reflects Natasha Romanoff's character framing and the film's critique of American intelligence agencies, but the traditional content is so strong at 25.6 that it carries a comfortable TRADITIONAL verdict.

#9. Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 18.3 WOKE: 3.9 MARGIN: +14.5 TRAD

Director: Joss Whedon • Stars: Robert Downey Jr., Chris Evans, Chris Hemsworth, Mark Ruffalo • Year: 2015

The film that critics dismissed as the weakest Avengers entry contains the single most traditionally grounded sequence in the entire franchise. Clint Barton, the Avenger everyone underestimates, has a pregnant wife named Laura, two kids, and a farmhouse in the middle of nowhere that nobody on the team knew about. He hides it. Not because he is ashamed, but because it is the one thing he cannot afford to lose, and the only way to protect it is to keep it outside the scope of what the Avengers do. When the team retreats to that farmhouse, shattered and doubting, it is Laura and the quiet reality of family life that restores them. Whedon includes Thor's cave vision and the Vision's birth and the Hulkbuster fight, but the farm is the film's moral center, and it is entirely traditional. A man protecting his family. A woman who chose this life knowing the cost. Children who see their father as a hero. At +14.5 traditional, Age of Ultron is far more conservative than its reputation suggests.

#11. The Avengers (2012)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 16.4 WOKE: 4.5 MARGIN: +11.9 TRAD

Director: Joss Whedon • Stars: Robert Downey Jr., Chris Evans, Chris Hemsworth, Mark Ruffalo • Year: 2012

Six heroes with competing egos, ideologies, and power sets assemble to stop an alien invasion of New York. They fight each other first. They find a way to work together. They win. That summary sounds simple now, but in 2012 nobody knew if an interconnected superhero universe could work at this scale. Whedon proved it could by grounding the spectacle in character conflict: Tony Stark's individualism versus Steve Rogers's collectivist duty, Thor's divine authority versus the team's secular humanism, Bruce Banner's fear of his own power versus the situation's demand that he use it. The death of Agent Coulson is the film's moral keystone: a good man dies, and his death becomes the catalyst that unites people who could not be united by argument alone. At +11.9 traditional with a 4.5 woke score, The Avengers represents the MCU at its most balanced: spectacle, character, and a moral framework that treats sacrifice as meaningful.

#13. Iron Man (2008)

TRADITIONAL LEAN TRAD: 14.4 WOKE: 5.0 MARGIN: +9.3 TRAD

Director: Jon Favreau • Stars: Robert Downey Jr., Gwyneth Paltrow, Jeff Bridges • Year: 2008

Seventeen years and dozens of sequels later, it is worth going back to the film that started it all. Iron Man is not merely historically significant. It is structurally conservative in ways that become more apparent with time. Tony Stark's origin is a conversion narrative: an arms dealer sees the human cost of his weapons, is held captive, builds his way out, and returns home with a conscience he did not possess before. The villain is his own business partner, a man who represents the corrupt corporate establishment that Stark himself enabled. The film's skepticism of the military-industrial complex lands across party lines, but Stark's arc is fundamentally about a man taking moral responsibility for the consequences of his actions and committing to repair the damage. The 5.0 woke score is low enough to keep this at TRADITIONAL LEAN, but the film's political content is anti-establishment rather than progressive in the contemporary sense.

TRADITIONAL LEAN TRAD: 14.0 WOKE: 5.4 MARGIN: +8.7 TRAD

Director: Scott Derrickson • Stars: Benedict Cumberbatch, Tilda Swinton, Chiwetel Ejiofor • Year: 2016

Doctor Strange is the MCU's most spiritually interesting film, and that is saying something given the franchise's general allergy to anything resembling genuine metaphysics. Credit goes almost entirely to Scott Derrickson, one of the only openly Christian filmmakers working at this budget level. Stephen Strange's journey from arrogant materialist to servant of a higher order is a conversion story in the classical mode: pride shattered, humility learned, purpose found. The Ancient One's final speech about time, meaning, and the acceptance of death is the most overtly spiritual material in any MCU film. The Eastern mysticism framing pushes the woke score to 5.4, but the underlying structure is conservative: a man submits to a tradition larger than himself, submits to discipline, and discovers that service is liberation.

#16. Spider-Man: Homecoming (2017)

TRADITIONAL LEAN TRAD: 13.0 WOKE: 6.2 MARGIN: +6.8 TRAD

Director: Jon Watts • Stars: Tom Holland, Michael Keaton, Robert Downey Jr. • Year: 2017

Jon Watts and his writers made the smart call and skipped the origin story entirely. You have already seen Uncle Ben die twice. Instead, Homecoming starts with Peter Parker already Spider-Man, already desperate for bigger action, already chafing at the smallness of his Queens neighborhood patrol. The film is a John Hughes high school comedy wearing a superhero suit, and it works because it takes Peter's adolescence seriously. His desire to prove himself to Tony Stark is not vanity. It is the desire of a fatherless boy to earn the approval of the closest thing to a father figure he has left. Michael Keaton's Vulture is the film's secret weapon: a working-class villain whose grievance against Stark Industries is not unreasonable, but whose methods are indefensible. The 6.2 woke score comes from the diverse high school cast and some contemporary teen dynamics, but the film's moral framework is solidly traditional at +6.8.

The MCU in Context

Twenty-one films spanning seventeen years. The pattern is clear and the data confirms it. The early MCU, from Iron Man through The Avengers and The Winter Soldier, built its moral framework on traditional values: self-sacrifice, earned power, protection of the innocent, personal responsibility, duty to something larger than yourself. Those films carry the highest traditional margins in the franchise.

The middle period, from Age of Ultron through Far From Home, introduced progressive content in measured doses that generally did not overwhelm the traditional architecture. The films from this era cluster in the TRADITIONAL and TRADITIONAL LEAN categories.

The later period tells a different story. Endgame loaded progressive signaling into its climactic battle sequence. Captain Marvel was built from the ground up as an ideological vehicle. Only The Fantastic Four: First Steps in 2026 has reversed the trend, delivering a traditional margin that recalls the franchise's earliest and cleanest entries.

The MCU is not ideologically uniform. The 41-point gap between Captain America: The First Avenger at +23.1 and Captain Marvel at -16.2 proves that the franchise contains multitudes.
