Apple spent over $6 billion on original content since launching Apple TV+ in 2019. The platform has positioned itself as the prestige streamer -- fewer titles, higher production values, more awards. But what does the full library look like through the VirtueVigil lens, with every title scored by the same dual-metric system?

The answer is more interesting than the "Apple is woke" or "Apple is fine" camps would have you believe. VirtueVigil has scored 16 Apple TV+ originals. None lands below -4 margin. Compare that to Netflix, which has Emilia Pérez at -25.4 and multiple WOKE-tier titles, or theatrical releases where STRONGLY WOKE is a recurring category. Apple's content strategy appears to avoid ideological extremes by design.

The TRADITIONAL and STRONGLY TRADITIONAL tiers include seven titles anchored by F1 (+31), Widow's Bay (+16), and the first season of Ted Lasso (+13).

Apple TV+ is not fighting a culture war. Its brand is prestige, and prestige in 2026 means progressive assumptions built into the storytelling rather than argued for. The ideological content is quieter, more embedded, and more consistent than on any other platform VirtueVigil tracks. The pattern is stable: the dramas lean woke, the action and genre content leans traditional, and the flagship shows sit in the mixed middle. #6. Napoleon (2023)

Woke Score: 9.5 • Traditional Score: 10.6 • Verdict: MIXED • Margin: +1 TRAD

Genre: Historical / Epic / Drama | Director: Ridley Scott | Year: 2023

Ridley Scott's Napoleon is a film at war with itself. The battle sequences -- Austerlitz, Borodino, Waterloo -- are extraordinary, among the best military set pieces Scott has ever staged. The portrait of Napoleon the man is deliberately, sometimes frustratingly, small: a petulant genius, a needy husband, a conqueror who conquered everything except his own insecurities. Joaquin Phoenix plays him with the same hollowed-out intensity he brought to the Joker, and the result is a film that refuses to be a conventional historical epic. Some critics accused Scott of reducing Napoleon to a domestic farce to serve a feminist reading of history. The film does devote substantial screen time to the Napoleon-Josephine relationship and frames the emperor's military ambition as an extension of masculine inadequacy. Whether that reading is valid or Scott simply wanted to demystify the myth is a question the film declines to answer. At 9.5 woke to 10.6 traditional, the +1 margin is the narrowest possible split. The film is whatever you bring to it.

The Takeaway

Apple TV+ occupies a unique position in the streaming landscape. The platform has no STRONGLY WOKE content. No WOKE content. The most woke title in the library -- Killers of the Flower Moon at -4 -- would rank outside the top 15 on Netflix's equivalent list. Apple's content strategy appears to have a quiet ideological ceiling.

But the pattern is unmistakable. The four WOKE LEAN titles share a common thread: progressive assumptions woven into the fabric of the storytelling rather than argued for explicitly. Killers of the Flower Moon frames history through the lens of white guilt. Blitz uses a WWII setting to prosecute 21st-century arguments about empire. Arrival assumes institutional authority is the problem and individual emotional truth is the answer. Silo Season 3 sharpens its anti-authoritarian edge. None of these films is a manifesto. All of them carry progressive freight.

The MIXED tier is where Apple TV+ distinguishes itself from competitors. Severance, Napoleon, Lucky, and Argylle all score within two points of zero margin in either direction. These are not fence-sitting titles. They are ambitious projects that happen to treat ideology as an ingredient rather than the main course. Severance in particular -- with season one at +2 and season two at -2 -- demonstrates that shows can engage with contemporary themes without becoming ideological vehicles.

The TRADITIONAL tier is where the platform's best content for families lives. Ted Lasso Season 1 (+13) proves that warmth and traditional values are not incompatible. Pluribus (+12) shows that prestige drama does not require progressive politics. Widow's Bay (+16) and Fight or Flight (+13) demonstrate that genre content works better when it respects audience expectations instead of subverting them. And F1 (+31) is simply one of the most traditionally-scored films in the entire VirtueVigil database -- a $300 million blockbuster that proves Hollywood can still make movies for everyone.

For parents making decisions about what streaming services to trust, Apple TV+ offers the safest library on the market by VirtueVigil metrics. No content below -4 margin. Nearly half the library (7 of 16) in TRADITIONAL or STRONGLY TRADITIONAL territory. And a MIXED middle that treats audience intelligence with respect. The platform is not perfect, but it is the closest thing to a safe harbor in the current streaming landscape.
