2023 was the year the culture war went fully mainstream in Hollywood, and the box office reflected it in ways studio executives are still trying to process. On one end, Barbie became a global phenomenon by dressing a feminist manifesto in pink plastic, grossing $1.4 billion. On the other, Sound of Freedom became the year's most politically charged surprise hit, earning $250 million while much of the press actively campaigned against it. Between these poles sat 59 other films in the VirtueVigil database, each scored on our VVWS dual-metric system, and together they paint a picture of an industry at war with itself and its audience.

The VirtueVigil Woke Score (VVWS) measures progressive ideological content: identity politics, institutional distrust, progressive sexual framing, and systemic oppression narratives. The Traditional Score measures traditional values: courage, family, faith, sacrifice, moral clarity, and individual agency. The margin between them is what matters. A negative margin means the film pushes progressive ideology. A positive margin means it affirms traditional values. The size of the gap tells you how hard it pushes.

Of the 61 scored 2023 films, 20 lean woke, 23 are roughly balanced, and 18 lean traditional. On raw numbers, that looks like Hollywood exercised some restraint. It did not. The woke films of 2023 received the bulk of the marketing spend, the awards campaign money, and the cultural oxygen. Poor Things, which lands at -37.1 WOKE as the most ideologically aggressive film of the year, won four Academy Awards. Barbie, at -32.1 WOKE, was nominated for eight. The traditional films, with a few notable exceptions, were treated by critics as disposable or dangerous. The 2023 pattern is not balance. It is a distribution problem: progressive films get the megaphone; traditional films have to fight for air.

Yorgos Lanthimos's adaptation of Alasdair Gray's novel is the most ideologically aggressive film in the 2023 VirtueVigil database by a wide margin, and it wears that distinction proudly. It is also a film whose entire project is to argue that civilization's restraints on appetite are a prison, and that liberation means tearing them all down. If you agree with that premise, this is a masterpiece. If you do not, it is 141 minutes of being told your values are the problem.

The most instructive comparison on this list is between Poor Things at #1 (-37.1, 4 Oscars, critical euphoria) and Sound of Freedom at #59 (+26.6, zero Oscars, critical hostility). Both are competently made films about liberation. One argues that sexual freedom is the highest good and that civilization's restraints are a prison. The other argues that protecting innocent children from predators is the highest good and that evil must be confronted directly. The institutions celebrated one and campaigned against the other. The marketplace made both successes. But only one of these films' values is treated as legitimate by the people who decide what gets made, what gets promoted, and what gets honored. The gap between what audiences value and what Hollywood values is now so wide that entire box office results are being treated as anomalies rather than data. They are not anomalies. They are a demand curve that the supply side is refusing to meet.
