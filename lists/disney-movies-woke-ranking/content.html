Disney is the most culturally dominant entertainment company on the planet. It owns Star Wars, Marvel, Pixar, and a century of animated classics that have shaped generations of childhoods. The VirtueVigil database now holds 32 Disney titles with completed Woke Scores, and the picture they paint is more revealing than any single review can capture. This is the full list, ranked from most woke to most traditional.

The spread is the story. At the top sits The Acolyte at a -60.8 margin (STRONGLY WOKE), a score so extreme it is in a category of its own. At the bottom sits Miracle at a +36.0 margin (STRONGLY TRADITIONAL). That is a 96.8-point range within a single company, which tells you Disney is not one thing. It is a collection of divisions and creative teams with radically different instincts, and the difference between what one team makes and another team makes is the difference between a lecture and a classic.

The distribution reveals a company that skews traditional in volume but carries an ideological vanguard that gets the headlines. One title lands STRONGLY WOKE, two WOKE, and three WOKE LEAN, for a total of six on the woke side of the ledger. Two titles land MIXED. On the traditional side: six TRADITIONAL LEAN, twelve TRADITIONAL, and six STRONGLY TRADITIONAL. That is 24 titles leaning traditional or stronger against 6 leaning woke or stronger. The numerical center of gravity is unmistakably traditional.

The pattern that jumps out first: Star Wars and Marvel titles bookend the entire list. The most woke Disney title is Star Wars (The Acolyte). The most traditional Disney title is a live-action sports drama (Miracle). The franchise that launched a thousand think-pieces about "getting political" spans from -60.8 to +13.2 within the same brand. The same company that made Captain Marvel also made Deadpool and Wolverine. The same studio that produced The Last Jedi also produced Rogue One. Disney is arguing with itself, and the argument is playing out in billion-dollar properties.

Parents do not need a decoder ring to navigate this. They need the scores. Here they are, ranked from most woke to most traditional.

Marriage, fatherhood, grief, and the discovery that the life you lived was the adventure all along: Pixar has never been more traditional than this.

#31: Toy Story (1995)

Pixar's first feature is 81 minutes long, and not a single one of those minutes wastes time on ideology. Toy Story is a deeply traditional story about the things that matter most: loyalty, sacrifice, and earning your place in the world. Woody begins as the favorite toy, secure in his position, threatened by the arrival of Buzz Lightyear. His arc is not about asserting his dominance. It is about learning that his worth is not measured by his position on the bed but by his willingness to serve the child who loves him and the toys who depend on him. Buzz's parallel arc, from delusional space ranger to toy who accepts his purpose, mirrors Woody's: meaning comes from fulfilling your role, not from believing you are something you are not. The woke score at 0.5 is effectively zero, and the 30.1 traditional score is earned by a film that believes in duty, community, and the sacred bond between a child and a toy. #32: Miracle (2004)

Woke Score: 1.4 • Traditional Score: 37.4 • Verdict: STRONGLY TRADITIONAL • Margin: +36.0

Platform: Disney+

Miracle has the easiest setup in sports film history. You already know the ending. The 1980 US Olympic hockey team beating the Soviet Union is one of the most famous moments in American sports, and the film cannot surprise you with the outcome. So it has to be about something else, and it is: the making of a team from a collection of individuals who had to learn that the name on the front of the jersey matters more than the name on the back. Kurt Russell's Herb Brooks is one of the great screen portrayals of American coaching: demanding, brutal, unapologetic, and driven by a love for his players that he will never say out loud because saying it would undermine the lesson. The 37.4 traditional score is the highest in the Disney database because the film's values, sacrifice for the collective, the subordination of ego to mission, the belief that Americans can do the impossible when they stop competing with each other and start competing together, are not just traditional. They are the tradition. The woke score is negligible because the film has nothing to say about politics and everything to say about character. Miracle is the standard against which every traditional score on this list is measured.

The Takeaway

Disney is not a monolith, and anyone who says otherwise is looking at a headline, not the data. The same company that produced The Acolyte at -60.8 also produced Miracle at +36.0. The same studio that made Captain Marvel also made Deadpool and Wolverine. The difference between those poles is not a subtle shading. It is the difference between two entirely different companies occupying the same balance sheet.

The pattern is clear: Star Wars and Marvel titles with new, diverse, or female-led characters cluster on the woke side, while legacy character stories, Pixar originals, and live-action dramas cluster on the traditional side. The woke titles get the media attention. The traditional titles get the audience loyalty. If you are choosing what to watch with your family, the bottom two-thirds of this list is where the real stories live, and the top third is where the messaging lives. That is not a value judgment from us. It is what the scores show.

Three titles deserve special mention. The Acolyte, at -60.8, is not just the most woke Disney title. It is the most ideologically aggressive mainstream production we have ever scored, a show where the progressive ideology is the entire product and the Star Wars branding is the wrapper. Toy Story, at +29.6, is the most traditional animated film in the database, a movie that built Pixar on the belief that your purpose is found in service to others. And Miracle, at +36.0, is the gold standard: a film about Americans learning to be Americans, with a near-zero woke score and a traditional score that says everything about what happens when you put the team before yourself.

The absence is telling. Only one Disney title lands in STRONGLY WOKE territory, and it was canceled after one season. The WOKE tier holds just two titles. The vast weight of Disney's output, 24 of 32 titles, leans traditional or stronger. The company's creative culture gets the headlines, but its catalog tells a different story: when Disney makes movies instead of statements, it lands on the traditional side of the ledger. The problem is that the statements dominate the conversation and drive away the audience.

One more thing worth noting: Pixar occupies a special place on this list. From Toy Story (1995) to Hoppers (2026), the studio's films cluster toward the traditional end, with Up, Toy Story 5, and the original Toy Story all landing STRONGLY TRADITIONAL. Even WALL-E, the studio's most political film, comes in at MIXED with a slight traditional lean. Pixar's storytelling DNA is fundamentally conservative: find your purpose, serve your community, earn your place, love the people you are given. When the studio remembers that, it makes masterpieces. When it forgets, it makes Hoppers, which is still a good film but a confused one.
