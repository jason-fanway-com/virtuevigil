The 2010s were the decade Hollywood lost the plot. The industry began the decade with The Hurt Locker winning Best Picture and ended it with Green Book, and the space between those two milestones maps a ten-year war over what movies were supposed to be. The cultural shift was not subtle. In 2010, a superhero movie about a scrawny kid from Brooklyn who becomes an avatar of American exceptionalism could be made without irony. By 2019, the studios were too afraid to even try. By the end of the decade, the industry's prestige apparatus was openly hostile to traditional values, and the audience had started to notice.

But here is the data. The 2010s also produced some of the most traditionally rich films in the entire VirtueVigil database. American Sniper, 13 Hours, Warrior, Fury, The Conjuring. These were not niche projects that slipped through the cracks. They were major studio releases that earned hundreds of millions of dollars precisely because they offered something the prestige pipeline refused to deliver: stories about courage, sacrifice, family duty, and moral clarity, told without a lecture attached. The market spoke. The critics often sneered. The audiences bought tickets.

VirtueVigil has reviewed more than 930 films and shows. Across the 2010s, 79 films in our database scored higher on traditional values than on woke ideology. The films on this list represent the top 20. Every single one of them had more to say about honor, sacrifice, and the bonds that hold families and nations together than about the political fashions of the moment. Rankings are determined by the VirtueVigil Woke Scoring System (VVWS): the Traditional Score measures traditional values present, the Woke Score measures progressive ideological content, and the Margin is the gap between them. A positive margin means the film's traditional content outweighs its progressive content. These are the films the decade tried to forget but the audience refused to. Ranked from most traditional to least.

#1 (Most Traditional): American Sniper (2014)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 36.6 WOKE: 2.5 MARGIN: +34 TRAD

Genre: Action, Biography, Drama • Year: 2014 • Platform: Theatrical (Warner Bros.)

Clint Eastwood's American Sniper is the highest-grossing war film in American history for a reason, and that reason is not complicated. The film tells the true story of Chris Kyle, the most lethal sniper in US military history, without apology and without irony. Chris Kyle is from Texas. He grows up hunting with his father, learning that there are three kinds of people: wolves, sheep, and sheepdogs. He becomes a SEAL, deploys four times, and accumulates 160 confirmed kills. The film does not interrogate him. It does not deconstruct him. It presents him as he was: a man who believed in what he was doing, who struggled with the moral weight of it, and who ultimately found his purpose in protecting the men beside him. With a traditional score of 36.6 and a woke score of 2.5, American Sniper is one of the most lopsidedly traditional films in the entire 930-review database. The critics who called it propaganda missed the point. The $547 million box office was the audience's answer.

#2: 13 Hours: The Secret Soldiers of Benghazi (2016)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 32.3 WOKE: 1.7 MARGIN: +31 TRAD

Genre: Action / War / Historical Drama • Year: 2016 • Platform: Theatrical

Michael Bay directing a film about Benghazi sounds like a punchline until you watch it. 13 Hours is the most restrained film Bay has ever made and one of the most effective. On September 11, 2012, Islamic militants attacked the US diplomatic mission in Benghazi, Libya. Six American security contractors, former special operators, defied orders to stand down and fought through the night to protect the lives of Americans who had been abandoned by the government that sent them there. The film does not play politics with the events. It stays in the compound, with the men on the ground, and it trusts the audience to draw their own conclusions. A traditional score of 32.3 and a woke score of 1.7 reflect a film that has exactly one interest: honoring the men who fought when nobody else would. The critics hated it. The veterans did not.

#3: Warrior (2011)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 30.9 WOKE: 0.7 MARGIN: +30 TRAD

Genre: Drama, Sports, Action • Year: 2011 • Platform: Theatrical

Warrior is the best sports film of the 2010s and it is not close. Gavin O'Connor's MMA drama about two estranged brothers competing in the same tournament is built on the oldest dramatic engine there is: a broken family trying to find its way back to wholeness. Tom Hardy and Joel Edgerton play Tommy and Brendan Conlon, sons of a recovering alcoholic father played by Nick Nolte, who gave one of the great late-career supporting performances. The film earns every emotional beat. The fighting is not spectacle. It is communication between people who have lost the ability to speak. A woke score of 0.7 means there is essentially no progressive ideology in the film. A traditional score of 30.9 comes from a story about forgiveness, masculine redemption, and the idea that the bonds of family are stronger than any wound. Warrior earned $23 million at the box office on a $25 million budget, a commercial failure, and it did not matter. It endures because it means what it says.

#4: Paddington 2 (2017)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 32.0 WOKE: 2.1 MARGIN: +30 TRAD

Genre: Family Comedy Adventure • Year: 2017 • Platform: Theatrical

Paddington 2 might be the most morally serious film of the 2010s. It does not feel like it. It is brightly colored and funny and stars a CGI bear who gets into scrapes with marmalade. But underneath all that, it is a film about the power of kindness to transform a community, about the way small acts of decency compound into something large, and about the irreducible worth of a single good soul. Paddington does not fight. He does not argue. He simply shows up, again and again, with grace and good cheer, and the world around him becomes better for it. A traditional score of 32.0 reflects a film where every moral signal points toward community, family, forgiveness, and simple decency. A woke score of 2.1 reflects a film with no interest in ideology whatsoever. The fact that this bear movie outranks almost every prestige drama of the decade on traditional values tells you something about the state of prestige drama.

#5: The Conjuring (2013)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 27.4 WOKE: 1.0 MARGIN: +26 TRAD

Genre: Horror, Supernatural • Year: 2013 • Platform: Streaming

The Conjuring is one of the most traditionally conservative horror films ever made by a major studio, and its $319 million global box office on a $20 million budget suggests audiences were hungry for exactly that. James Wan's film, based on the case files of real-life Catholic paranormal investigators Ed and Lorraine Warren, is built on a premise that would not survive a single development meeting today: a traditional married couple, devout in their faith, confronts demonic evil in defense of a family that cannot defend itself. The Warrens are not deconstructed or subverted. They are presented as heroes precisely because of their faith, their marriage, and their commitment to protecting the innocent. A traditional score of 27.4 reflects a film where prayer is a weapon, the nuclear family is the thing worth saving, and evil is real and must be confronted. A woke score of 1.0 reflects a film with zero interest in progressive politics.

#6: Fury (2014)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 31.1 WOKE: 4.9 MARGIN: +26 TRAD

Genre: War / Drama • Year: 2014 • Platform: Theatrical

Fury is a war film made by someone who actually cares about war. Not the spectacle of it. The weight of it. David Ayer spent years researching this project, talking to veterans and studying the European theater in the final months of fighting. The result is a film about the moral cost of combat that refuses to flinch. Brad Pitt plays Don "Wardaddy" Collier, a tank commander who has been fighting so long that the war has become indistinguishable from his identity. His crew, played by Shia LaBeouf, Michael Pena, and Jon Bernthal, are morally compromised men who have found in each other something like a family, forged in the only place such bonds can be forged: shared sacrifice under fire. A traditional score of 31.1 comes from a story where duty, brotherhood, and the willingness to die for the man beside you are the only currencies that matter. A woke score of 4.9 is barely visible.

#7: The Equalizer (2014)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 25.9 WOKE: 0.7 MARGIN: +25 TRAD

Genre: Action, Thriller, Crime • Year: 2014 • Platform: Theatrical

The Equalizer is a film about a man who knows the right thing to do and does it, at considerable personal cost, with extraordinary precision. That is the whole movie, and it works because Denzel Washington is not a simple actor playing a simple role. Robert McCall is a retired black-ops operative who works at a Home Depot-style hardware store by day and reads classic literature in a diner at night. When a young woman he barely knows is brutalized by the Russian mafia, he does not call the police. He does not organize a protest. He walks into a room full of bad men and makes sure they never hurt anyone again. A traditional score of 25.9 comes from a story about righteous violence in defense of the innocent, the oldest moral framework in action cinema. A woke score of 0.7 reflects a film with absolutely no interest in progressive political messaging. This is a film about a man, a code, and a hammer.

#8: Captain America: The First Avenger (2011)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 26.9 WOKE: 3.8 MARGIN: +23 TRAD

Genre: Action / Adventure / Superhero • Year: 2011 • Platform: Theatrical

Captain America: The First Avenger came out in 2011, before the MCU became an assembly line, and it is still the most traditionally American film the franchise has ever produced. Fifteen years of Marvel content later, nothing has touched it. Steve Rogers is a scrawny kid from Brooklyn who wants to serve his country not because he hates anyone but because he cannot stand by while bullies hurt people. The super-soldier serum does not change who he is. It reveals who he already was. The film has a clear-eyed sense of duty, sacrifice, and the moral responsibility that comes with power. A traditional score of 26.9 reflects a superhero film that treats patriotism as a virtue rather than a punchline. A woke score of 3.8 is the lowest of any MCU entry. The franchise would never make a film this uncomplicatedly traditional again.

#9: Lone Survivor (2013)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 25.3 WOKE: 2.5 MARGIN: +23 TRAD

Genre: War / Action / Biography • Year: 2013 • Platform: Theatrical

Peter Berg made Lone Survivor because Marcus Luttrell asked him to. That origin matters. This is not a Hollywood producer's decision to exploit military sacrifice for box office returns. It is a filmmaker honoring a promise to a man who lost his entire team in Afghanistan during Operation Red Wings. The film follows four Navy SEALs on a mission to capture or kill a Taliban commander. What happens next is a story of courage, brotherhood, and the kind of sacrifice that makes it difficult to leave the theater unchanged. Berg does not editorialize. He shows what happened, and he trusts the audience to honor it. A traditional score of 25.3 comes from a film about men who give their lives for each other without hesitation. A woke score of 2.5 reflects the near-total absence of political messaging.

#10: The Lion King (2019)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 25.3 WOKE: 2.7 MARGIN: +23 TRAD

Genre: Musical / Drama / Adventure • Year: 2019 • Platform: Theatrical

Disney's 2019 The Lion King is a technically stunning but emotionally hollow recreation of one of the most traditionally structured stories in the studio's catalog. The photorealistic animation is jaw-dropping as pure technology, but the realistic animal faces cannot convey the emotional range of hand-drawn animation. Still, the story survives the translation. A father dies to save his son. A son runs from his responsibility and must be called back by the memory of the father. The rightful king must reclaim his place from a usurper who has corrupted the kingdom. This is not subtle stuff and it was never meant to be. A traditional score of 25.3 comes from a story about inheritance, duty, and the idea that there is a natural order worth defending. A woke score of 2.7 reflects the fact that even Disney, in 2019, could not figure out how to inject progressive politics into The Lion King.

#11: Dunkirk (2017)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 24.1 WOKE: 1.7 MARGIN: +22 TRAD

Genre: War / Historical Drama • Year: 2017 • Platform: Theatrical

Christopher Nolan made Dunkirk at the height of his creative freedom and chose to strip war of everything Hollywood has spent decades adding. There is no love story. No ideological framing. No politics at all. Just 400,000 men trapped on a beach, the civilian boats crossing the Channel to rescue them, and the Spitfire pilots burning fuel they do not have to protect the evacuation below. Nolan's structural trick, interweaving three timelines that converge at the climax, is not just formal cleverness. It is a way of saying that individual acts of courage, separated by space and time, combine into something larger than any of them. A traditional score of 24.1 comes from a film about duty, courage, and the idea that a nation is defined by what its people will do for each other when everything is on the line. A woke score of 1.7 reflects the complete absence of ideological messaging.

#12: Midway (2019)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 22.8 WOKE: 0.7 MARGIN: +22 TRAD

Genre: War / Action / Historical Drama • Year: 2019 • Platform: Theatrical

The Battle of Midway is one of the most improbable military victories in American history. Four Japanese fleet carriers sunk in a single day. A numerically and technologically inferior American force breaking the back of Japanese naval power six months after Pearl Harbor. Roland Emmerich, best known for disaster spectacles like Independence Day and The Day After Tomorrow, approaches the material with uncharacteristic sobriety. The film is not subtle. It is not interested in complicating the heroism with modern moral equivocation. A traditional score of 22.8 comes from a story about American sailors and pilots who flew into near-certain death because the alternative was worse. A woke score of 0.7 reflects a film with essentially no progressive content. This is pure historical tribute, straightforward and unapologetic, the kind of war film Hollywood made regularly before it decided it was too sophisticated for them.

#14: Creed (2015)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 24.6 WOKE: 2.7 MARGIN: +22 TRAD

Ryan Coogler took one of Hollywood's most sacred franchises and made it matter again. The Rocky series had been diminishing returns for decades, and the idea of a spinoff centered on Apollo Creed's illegitimate son sounded like a desperate cash grab until it was not. Michael B. #15: Man of Steel (2013)

Zack Snyder's Man of Steel was and remains controversial, but the controversy is about tone, not values. This is a film about a father who sends his only son to Earth to save him from a dying world, a son raised by two Kansas parents who teach him that his powers come with a moral obligation to protect, and the lonely burden of being the most powerful being on the planet. The flashback structure, criticized by some as disjointed, is in fact the film's thesis: Clark Kent became Superman not because of Krypton but because of Jonathan and Martha Kent. #17: John Wick: Chapter 2 (2017)

John Wick: Chapter 2 is a better film than it has any business being. The first John Wick was a stripped-down revenge story that worked on pure execution. This sequel expands the mythology while retaining the thing that made the original worth watching: a man who operates by a code, in a world where codes still mean something. Shutter Island grossed $294 million worldwide and holds an 82% Rotten Tomatoes score. The film has endured because of its refusal to provide easy answers. Martin Scorsese's adaptation of Dennis Lehane's novel is a psychological thriller about US Marshal Teddy Daniels, investigating a disappearance at a hospital for the criminally insane, who may or may not be a patient himself. #20: A Quiet Place (2018)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 22.5 WOKE: 2.9 MARGIN: +20 TRAD

Genre: Horror / Sci-Fi / Drama • Year: 2018 • Platform: Theatrical

A Quiet Place is, without question, one of the most pro-family mainstream studio films of the 2010s. That is not a political argument. It is a description of what the movie is about. The premise is clean and merciless: monsters with no eyes hunt by sound, and the Abbott family survives in silence. John Krasinski directs and stars alongside Emily Blunt as parents who have built a life for their children in a world where a single noise can get everyone killed. The horror mechanics are exceptional, but what makes the film matter is what those mechanics protect. Every silent gesture, every soundproofed room, every agonizing decision is in service of keeping a family together and alive. A traditional score of 22.5 comes from a film where parental sacrifice, family unity, and the willingness to die for your children are not themes but the entire foundation of the story. A woke score of 2.9 is barely detectable.

Twenty films. Seventeen score TRADITIONAL. Three score TRADITIONAL LEAN. Zero score woke. That is not a coincidence. It is a market signal that Hollywood spent the entire decade ignoring.

What is striking about this list is not just the films on it. It is what is missing. No Oscar winners for Best Picture appear. A handful of war films, a superhero entry, a horror thriller, a family comedy about a bear, and a Christian drama about forgiveness. These are the films the 2010s actually produced when it was not trying to lecture its audience. The industry's prestige apparatus rewarded something very different, and the gap between what the Academy celebrated and what the audience actually valued grew wider every year.

The 2010s were the decade Hollywood chose ideology over storytelling, and the consequences are now visible in collapsing box office returns and fractured audience trust. But these 20 films prove that the counter-current was always there. American Sniper made $547 million. The Conjuring made $319 million on a $20 million budget. A Quiet Place launched a franchise. The audience was never the problem. The industry was.
