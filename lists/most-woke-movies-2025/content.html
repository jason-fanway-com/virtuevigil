2025 was the year Hollywood stopped pretending.

For years, the industry's ideological content was delivered in code. Subtext. Allegory. A character's offhand remark that you could miss if you were looking at your phone. But in 2025, the mask came off. Studios and streamers released films and series that did not bother to disguise their progressive commitments. They embedded their politics in the premise, the dialogue, the moral architecture of the story itself, and dared audiences to complain.

VirtueVigil reviewed 135 films and series from 2025. Of those, 41 scored more woke than traditional. That is not a majority, but it is the most aggressive concentration of woke content of any year since we started scoring. The top 20 on this list did not simply lean left. They ran toward it at full speed, with budgets, star power, and cultural machinery behind them.

This list ranks the 20 most woke films and series of 2025, sorted from most woke to least. Every entry links to its full VirtueVigil review with complete scoring breakdown, trope audit, and parental guidance. None of these rankings are editorial opinion. They are the output of the VVWS dual-metric system: Severity x Authenticity x Centrality, applied consistently to every title in our database.

#1 (Most Woke): Zootopia 2 (2025)

STRONGLY WOKE MARGIN: -38 WOKE

Genre: Animation • Platform: Theatrical • Rating: NR

Zootopia 2 takes the crown as the most woke film of 2025 by an enormous margin. The original Zootopia was described by its own directors as being about racism, sexism, and governing through fear. The sequel turns every knob to eleven. With a woke score of 91.4 against a traditional score of 53.0, the film is not merely progressive. It is a comprehensive ideological delivery system wrapped in stunning animation. The allegory is so direct, so relentless, and so self-congratulatory about its own moral clarity that parents should know exactly what they are walking into: a Disney film that treats elementary-school children as political subjects first and audience members second.

#2: Bugonia (2025)

STRONGLY WOKE MARGIN: -20 WOKE

Genre: Black Comedy / Sci-Fi Thriller • Platform: Theatrical • Rating: NR

Yorgos Lanthimos's Bugonia is a skillfully made, darkly funny, and ultimately nihilistic black comedy thriller that wraps a radical anti-capitalist, environmentalist manifesto inside a genre-bending alien conspiracy kidnapping story. Two conspiracy-obsessed men, beekeeper Teddy Gatz (Jesse Plemons) and his cousin Don (Aidan Delbis), kidnap pharmaceutical CEO Michelle Fuller (Emma Stone), convinced she is an alien. The film treats corporate America as a parasite, environmental activism as the only moral position, and anyone who disagrees as either duped or complicit. Lanthimos is a gifted filmmaker, and Bugonia is never boring. But it is a sermon dressed as sci-fi, and the sermon is louder than the story.

#3: Eddington (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -16 WOKE

Genre: Western / Dark Comedy / Thriller • Platform: Theatrical • Rating: R • Director: Ari Aster

Ari Aster has been working his way toward a target for three films. Hereditary destroyed the family as a unit of safety. Midsommar destroyed the romantic relationship. Beau Is Afraid destroyed the male self. Eddington completes the project by destroying the conservative community. Joaquin Phoenix plays Sheriff Joe Cross in a small New Mexico town whose population of Trump-voting eccentrics becomes the canvas for Aster's most explicitly political film. The ensemble (Emma Stone, Pedro Pascal, Austin Butler) is extraordinary. The craft is undeniable. The argument, that conservative communities are fundamentally corrupt and deserve what is coming to them, is delivered without a trace of doubt or charity.

#4: Mother Mary (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -17 WOKE

Genre: Psychological Drama / Thriller / Music • Platform: Theatrical • Rating: NR

David Lowery's first major engagement with the machinery of celebrity and pop music is a film about power, intimacy, and the cost of being watched. Anne Hathaway plays Mother Mary, an iconic pop star whose career has been built on image, control, and the careful management of her persona. Michaela Coel plays Sam Anselm, a fashion designer and Mother Mary's estranged creative partner. The film treats their relationship as a crucible of queer identity, artistic authenticity, and the impossibility of female self-actualization under the male gaze. The craft is exquisite. Lowery is one of the most interesting filmmakers working. But the film's ideological framework, that traditional institutions constrain and harm everyone they touch, is never questioned, never complicated, never granted even a moment of internal doubt.

#5: Clown in a Cornfield (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -19 WOKE

Genre: Horror • Platform: Theatrical (RLJE Films / Shudder) • Rating: NR

Clown in a Cornfield is a Woke Trap in its purest form. The first half is a competent, engaging slasher about a teenager from Philadelphia who moves to a dying Missouri town and discovers something sinister beneath the surface. The second half reveals what that something is: conservative small-town America, depicted as a murderous cult whose ideology is the real monster. The film is well-made. The scares work. But it structures itself as a bait-and-switch, luring horror fans with genre pleasures and then delivering a political lecture that treats anyone who lives between the coasts as potentially homicidal. At -19 margin, it is the most ideologically aggressive horror film of the year.

#6: KPop Demon Hunters (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -6 WOKE

Genre: Animated Musical Urban Fantasy • Platform: Netflix • Rating: NR

Evaluating KPop Demon Hunters requires two entirely separate tracks. As a piece of entertainment, it is an unqualified triumph. The animation is gorgeous, the musical numbers are genuinely thrilling, and the premise, three young women form a globally famous K-pop group that secretly hunts demons in Seoul, is as inventive as anything released in 2025. The traditional content (the demons are genuinely evil, the heroism is real, the friendships feel earned) keeps it from scoring higher, but at -6 woke, KPop Demon Hunters is a reminder that the most effective delivery system for ideology is not the lecture. #7: Adolescence (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -8 WOKE

Genre: Psychological Crime Drama • Platform: Netflix • Rating: NR

Adolescence is the most important piece of television produced in 2025, and that is not the same thing as saying it is the best. Armed police raid a family home in South Yorkshire at dawn and drag 13-year-old Jamie Miller out of bed. He is arrested on suspicion of murdering his classmate Katie Leonard, a girl who rejected his romantic advances and then cyberbullied him. The series is technically extraordinary: each episode is a single continuous take, and the performances are so naturalistic they feel surveilled rather than performed. The ideological content is in the frame the series puts around its story. Adolescence treats Jamie's crime not as the act of a disturbed individual but as the logical endpoint of toxic masculinity, online radicalization, and a culture that teaches boys they are entitled to female attention. It is riveting television that also functions as a progressive diagnostic tool.

#8: Presence (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -14 WOKE

Genre: Horror / Thriller • Platform: Theatrical • Rating: NR

Presence is the most technically inventive horror film of early 2025 and also a quietly progressive film wearing an arthouse horror mask. Steven Soderbergh shoots the entire film from the ghost's point of view: the camera drifts through rooms, watches characters from doorways, hovers protectively over the family's neglected daughter Chloe. The film's surface is a haunted house story. Its interior is a family drama about the emotional abandonment of a teenage girl by her distracted, careerist parents, and the supernatural force that steps in to fill the void. The traditional elements are present (the ghost is a protective presence, not a malevolent one), but the film's moral framework, that the nuclear family fails children and outsiders must intervene, is delivered with Soderbergh's characteristic formal precision and zero interest in alternative readings.

#9: Wednesday: Season 2 (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -6 WOKE

Genre: Gothic Mystery / Dark Comedy • Platform: Netflix • Rating: TV-14 • Director: Tim Burton, Gandja Monteiro, Victor Gaudioso, Paco Cabezas

The first season of Wednesday was a cultural phenomenon, becoming the second-most watched English-language Netflix series ever. It succeeded because it understood its core asset: Jenna Ortega playing Wednesday Addams as a genuinely alien intelligence navigating a world she finds stupid, and discovering that the world has things in it worth protecting despite herself. Season 2 keeps the gothic aesthetic and Ortega's note-perfect performance but tilts harder into progressive territory. The season's new characters, its treatment of the Nevermore outcast metaphor, and its insistence that the real monsters are the conservative normals who fear difference, push the show from the traditional-lean territory of Season 1 into a woke lean. Still enormously entertaining. Less ideologically neutral than before.

#10: Avatar: Fire and Ash (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -9 WOKE

Genre: Epic Sci-Fi Action Adventure • Platform: Theatrical • Rating: NR

James Cameron has now made three Avatar films. Each one is technically extraordinary and ideologically predictable. Fire and Ash is both the best-looking film of 2025 and an almost proud repetition of every idea the franchise put on the table in 2009: anti-military messaging, noble-indigenous-versus-corporate-colonizers, and a white protagonist who becomes the best version of the culture he was sent to destroy. The Na'vi remain the most expensive political metaphor in cinema history. Cameron is a genius of spectacle, and Fire and Ash contains sequences that no other living director could execute. But the moral universe of Avatar has not grown in complexity across three films and sixteen years. It has only grown louder and more expensive.

#11: The White Lotus: Season 3 (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -7 WOKE

Genre: Dark Comedy Drama • Platform: HBO / Max • Rating: NR

Mike White's third season of his luxury-resort mortality play takes the show to Thailand, raises the body count's spiritual ambitions, and arrives at roughly the same place the previous two seasons arrived: the rich are hollow, the beautiful destroy each other, and nobody learns anything. The thesis is not without value. White Lotus at its best is genuinely satirical, and when it connects, the show is as sharp as anything on television. But Season 3 sharpens the show's progressive framework. Buddhism is borrowed for vibes while Western progressive values are treated as the only coherent moral system. The Thai characters exist to teach the white guests lessons about spirituality and decolonization. At -7 woke, White Lotus remains the most elegantly filmed progressive seminar on television, which is a compliment and a warning in equal measure.

#12: The Wedding Banquet (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -15 WOKE

Genre: Romantic Comedy • Platform: Theatrical (Bleecker Street) • Rating: NR

The original 1993 Wedding Banquet was a film about a gay man living a double life, caught between the love he had chosen and the family expectations he had not been able to refuse. It was genuinely moving because it took the parents seriously. They were not villains. They loved their son and wanted grandchildren and did not know that their happiness depended on their son's silence about who he was. The remake does not extend that charity. The parents are obstacles to be overcome rather than people to be loved. The film's progressive framework is totalizing and impatient. At -15 woke, the 2025 Wedding Banquet is a fascinating artifact: a remake that proves how much the culture has changed by losing everything that made the original great.

#13: Squid Game: Season 3 (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -6 WOKE

Genre: Survival Thriller / Dystopian Drama • Platform: Netflix • Rating: TV-MA • Director: Hwang Dong-hyuk

Squid Game Season 3 is the conclusion Hwang Dong-hyuk was always building toward. It is bleak, morally committed, and technically excellent. Whether you find it satisfying depends almost entirely on whether you share his politics. The season picks up after the catastrophic end of Season 2, with Gi-hun's rebellion failed and his best friend dead. The remaining episodes push the show's anti-capitalist critique past allegory and into manifesto territory. The rich are irredeemable. The system is designed to produce suffering. The only moral response is total resistance. The craft is unimpeachable. Lee Jung-jae and Lee Byung-hun deliver career-best work. But the season's refusal to grant the audience even a sliver of ambiguity about its political convictions makes Season 3 the most ideologically assertive chapter of the most-watched non-English series in Netflix history.

#14: The Ugly Stepsister (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -7 WOKE

Genre: Satirical Black Comedy / Body Horror • Platform: Theatrical • Rating: R • Director: Emilie Blichfeldt

Emilie Blichfeldt looked at Cinderella and asked the question nobody wanted answered: what would the ugly stepsister's story actually feel like if you told it honestly? The answer involves tapeworms, butcher knives, and more blood than a Dario Argento retrospective. Lea Myren plays Elvira, the stepsister determined to win the prince by any means necessary, including surgical self-mutilation. The film is a satirical black comedy about female beauty standards, patriarchal competition, and the violence women are taught to inflict on themselves to be worthy of love. It is imaginative, formally daring, and wickedly funny. It is also a film whose entire moral argument is that traditional femininity is a prison designed by men. That is not subtext. It is the text, delivered at maximum volume and with maximum gore.

#15: Now You See Me: Now You Don't (2025)

WOKE MARGIN: -11 WOKE

Genre: Thriller • Platform: Theatrical • Rating: NR

The Now You See Me franchise has always been about magicians stealing from the rich and giving to the poor. But the third installment drops the magic-as-metaphor pretense and treats theft as straightforward justice. The film moralizes redistribution, anti-elite resentment, and climate scolding as the natural order of things, wrapping it all in the glossy, crowd-pleasing heist spectacle the franchise is known for. The core feel-good engine is not clever magic. It is the conviction that the wealthy deserve to lose everything and the audience should cheer while it happens. At -11 woke, Now You See Me: Now You Don't is a heist film where the real theft is of the audience's critical distance.

#16: Death of a Unicorn (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -5 WOKE

Genre: Dark Fantasy / Comedy Horror • Platform: Theatrical / HBO Max • Rating: NR

Death of a Unicorn does not try to hide its politics. It is an anti-capitalist satire wrapped in a creature feature, and the rich people are cartoonishly evil. The young progressive woman is the moral compass. The unicorns are nature's revenge on corporate greed. If you have seen Jurassic Park, Alien, or any film where a corporation tries to weaponize a creature it does not understand, you know the beats. The difference is that Death of a Unicorn uses those beats not as genre pleasures but as ideological ones. The film is well-made, funny in places, and the creature design is impressive. But at -5 woke, it is a film whose entertainment value is inseparable from the satisfaction it expects you to feel watching the wealthy get what they deserve, a satisfaction the film considers morally self-evident.

#17: It Was Just an Accident (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -4 WOKE

Genre: Thriller / Drama • Platform: Theatrical (Neon) • Rating: NR

Jafar Panahi's It Was Just an Accident is a taut, morally complex thriller about former Iranian political prisoners who kidnap a man they believe tortured them in jail. It won the Palme d'Or at Cannes 2025 and earned Oscar nominations for Best Original Screenplay and Best International Feature Film. The film is exquisitely crafted, darkly funny, and unflinching in its depiction of how state violence deforms both victims and perpetrators. The woke score reflects the film's political framework: the Iranian regime is treated as irredeemably evil, Western democratic values are implicitly held up as the alternative, and the moral complexity the film earns is entirely within a framework of state-as-oppressor. It is a great film made by a dissident who has earned the right to his anger. It is also a film whose politics are unambiguous and whose Western champions will conveniently ignore that the hero they are celebrating looks nothing like their own domestic politics.

#18: Zero Day (2025)

MIXED MARGIN: -1 WOKE

Genre: Drama / Thriller / Political • Platform: Netflix • Rating: TV-MA • Director: Multiple directors

Zero Day is the first MIXED entry on this list, and it earns that classification honestly. Robert De Niro plays George Mullen, a former president recalled to lead an investigation after a catastrophic cyberattack on American infrastructure. The series is a political thriller that wants to be the definitive post-9/11 drama for the current moment. De Niro and Jesse Plemons are excellent. Lizzy Caplan is excellent. The show's problem is not craft but conviction: it wants to critique both sides of the partisan divide while clearly believing one side is fundamentally more dangerous. The result is a series that attempts even-handedness while its narrative machinery quietly stacks the deck. At -1 woke, Zero Day is the most ideologically conflicted entry on this list, which makes it the most interesting.

#19: If I Had Legs I'd Kick You (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -4 WOKE

Genre: Drama / Dark Comedy • Platform: Theatrical (A24) • Rating: NR

Rose Byrne delivers a performance so raw, so physically committed, and so mercilessly honest that it stops being acting and starts feeling like surveillance footage of a person's worst year. She plays Linda, a psychotherapist who cannot keep her own life from collapsing. Her daughter has a pediatric condition that requires constant care. Her husband is absent. Her patients' crises echo her own. The film is a portrait of a woman being crushed by the expectations of motherhood, career, and marriage, and its argument is unambiguous: the structures that demand women perform all three roles simultaneously are the real antagonist. At -4 woke, If I Had Legs I'd Kick You is a tour de force of acting that also functions as an indictment of traditional family structures, delivered by one of the most interesting distributors in the business.

#20: Andor - Season 2 (2025)

WOKE LEAN MARGIN: -5 WOKE

Genre: Sci-Fi & Fantasy • Platform: Disney+ • Rating: NR

Andor Season 2 is probably the finest piece of television Disney has produced under the Star Wars banner, and it is, without reservation, the most explicitly political. These two facts are related. Tony Gilroy and his writers have made a show about the birth of a rebellion against an authoritarian empire, and they have made it with the kind of narrative rigor and moral seriousness that franchise entertainment almost never achieves. The show's politics are clear: fascism is evil, collective action is the only response, and the people who profit from oppression are as culpable as the people who order the atrocities. None of that is controversial in a Star Wars context. What pushes Andor into woke-lean territory is the show's insistence on mapping its fictional politics onto real-world frameworks so explicitly that the allegory stops being allegory. It is a masterpiece. It is also a progressive political education wearing stormtrooper armor.
