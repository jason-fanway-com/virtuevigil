Horror has always been the genre that says the quiet parts out loud. When a culture is anxious about something, horror films show up to exploit that anxiety. In the 1950s, it was radiation and communist infiltration. In the 1970s, it was the collapse of the nuclear family. In the 2020s, it is progressive ideology wearing a slasher's mask and demanding you call it entertainment.

The woke horror film is a specific phenomenon. It does not merely contain progressive ideas. It uses the genre's tools, fear, revulsion, catharsis, to advance an ideological argument. The monster is the patriarchy. The church is the villain. The final girl's real enemy was traditional values all along. These films do not just scare you. They lecture you. And they are often very good at it, which makes them more dangerous, not less.

VirtueVigil has reviewed 109 horror films with full VVWS scores. Of those, 20 land on the wrong side of the ledger, waking harder than they horrify. This list ranks them by woke margin: the gap between Woke Score (everything the film loads in from the progressive ideological playbook) and Traditional Score (everything it gets right about courage, sacrifice, honor, duty, and faith). The larger the negative margin, the more the film's ideology overwhelms its craft. Each entry links to the complete VirtueVigil review with methodology breakdowns, content warnings, and parental guidance. Ranked most woke first.

This is not a list of bad movies. Several of these films are technically excellent. That is the problem. The most effective propaganda does not announce itself. It entertains you while it works.

#1: Heretic

WOKE WOKE: 41.2 TRAD: 13.7 MARGIN: -27.5 WOKE

Genre: Horror • Platform: Streaming • Year: 2024

Heretic is not a horror film that happens to touch on religion. It is a theological assault on Christianity, and on all monotheistic faith, dressed in genre clothing and delivered via Hugh Grant's most charming, murderous performance yet. Two young Mormon missionaries knock on the wrong door and find themselves trapped in a house where faith is systematically dismantled through a series of escalating intellectual and physical tortures. The film is well-made, well-acted, and ideologically vicious. Grant's character is framed as a rationalist hero whose only sin is pushing too hard against delusion. The missionaries' faith is presented as naive at best, complicit in global evil at worst. Heretic earned the highest woke score of any horror film in the VirtueVigil database for a reason: it does not want to scare you. It wants to convince you that religious belief is a form of mental illness.

#2: The Bride!

WOKE WOKE: 34.8 TRAD: 10.4 MARGIN: -24.4 WOKE

Genre: Gothic Romance / Horror • Platform: Theatrical (IMAX) • Year: 2026

Maggie Gyllenhaal's The Bride! tells you exactly what it is from the first frame, and it does not care if you approve. Set in 1930s Chicago, the film reimagines the Bride of Frankenstein as a feminist liberation story in which the monster is not the creature but the men who created her. Christian Bale plays Frankenstein's monster. Jessie Buckley plays the bride. The film is visually striking, musically inventive, and ideologically relentless. Every male character is either predatory, pathetic, or both. The bride's journey from constructed object to autonomous subject is the entire film. The traditional gothic horror that gave the original its power, the genuine awe at creation, the moral weight of playing God, is replaced with a straightforward message: men are the real monsters. IMAX screens have rarely looked this good while saying this little.

#3: The Substance

WOKE WOKE: 37.3 TRAD: 13.2 MARGIN: -24.1 WOKE

Genre: Horror • Platform: Streming • Year: 2024

The Substance is body horror as feminist manifesto. Demi Moore plays an aging actress who takes a black-market drug that generates a younger version of herself, played by Margaret Qualley. The rules: each gets one week in the body, then must switch. The younger self predictably refuses to cede control and the body horror escalates to Cronenbergian extremes. The film has been celebrated as a brilliant satire of Hollywood's treatment of women. What it actually is: a 140-minute argument that beauty standards are male violence, that the male gaze is literally lethal, and that female self-destruction is always someone else's fault. The craft is undeniable. Coralie Fargeat directs with complete confidence. The practical effects are the best of 2024. But the entire movie runs on a single ideological engine that never idles and never questions itself. It is a polemic dressed as a midnight movie.

#4: Immaculate

WOKE WOKE: 25.4 TRAD: 3.5 MARGIN: -21.9 WOKE

Genre: Horror • Platform: Theatrical / Hulu • Year: 2024

Sydney Sweeney spent years developing Immaculate as a vehcle that would let her operate in the horror genre while carrying a message she clearly believed in. The film that emerged, shot in Italy, dressed in Catholic iconography, structured as Rosemary's Baby with nuns, is a straightforward indictment of the Catholic Church as a patriarchal prison that controls women's bodies. Sweeney plays a young American nun at an Italian convent who mysteriously becomes pregnant. The Church declares it a miracle. It is not. The film's thesis: religious institutions do not protect women. They consume them. The trad score of 3.5 is one of the lowest in the database for a reason: there is no countervailing traditional content here. The church is purely predatory. Faith is purely delusion. The film ends exactly where you expect it to, with the same argument that horror has been making about the Catholic Church since the 1970s, delivered with more blood and less ambiguity.

#5: Levitcus

WOKE WOKE: 23.8 TRAD: 4.0 MARGIN: -19.8 WOKE

Genre: Horror / Romance / Coming-of-Age • Platform: Theatrical / Streming • Director: Adian Chiarella • Year: 2026

Levitcus is a competent horror film with a very clear ideological mission: to argue that religious communities opposed to homosexuality are not just wrong, but monstrous. The setup is familiar coming-of-age territory. Naim is the new kid, moving to a small Australian town with his mother after a family tragedy. He befriends a boy named Hunter, and the two develop a romantic relationship that the town's evangelical Christian community responds to with escalating violence, culminating in a literal exorcism. The film's title announces the argument before the first frame: Levitcus 20:13, the biblical prohbition on homosexual acts, is the real horror. Religiously devout people are portrayed uniformly as violent bigots. The only sympathetic characters are those who reject faith entirely. A trad score of 4.0 tells you everything: there is nothing here for a traditional audience to hold onto.

#6: Clown in a Cornfield

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 23.5 TRAD: 4.9 MARGIN: -18.6 WOKE

Genre: Horror • Platform: Theatrical (RLJE Films / Shuder) • Year: 2025

Clown in a Cornfield is a competently made slasher with a clear political agenda that it conceals until past the midpoint and then delivers with zero ambiguity. The first half delivers genre mechanics exactly as promised: a small town, a creepy clown, inventive kills. The second half reveals that the real villain is not the clown but the town's conservative adults, who have been systematically eliminating the progressive youth who threaten their traditionalist control. The film is an adaptation of a young adult novel and it shows: the moral universe is one where kids are always right, adults are always wrong, and the only legitimate authority is teenage moral clarity. If you enjoy slasher genre mechanics, the first half delivers. If you care about what the film is actually arguing, the second half will make you angry.

#7: Midsommar

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 20.6 TRAD: 2.4 MARGIN: -18.2 WOKE

Genre: Horror / Drama • Platform: Theatrical • Director: Ari Aster • Year: 2019

Midsommar is a genuinely good film. That is the most important thing to say before everything else, because it shapes how dangerous it is. If Midsommar were a crude feminist horror film, if the cult were obviously evil from the first scene and the protagonist's journey were framed as a straightforward cautionary tale, it would be easy to dismiss. But Ari Aster is more sophisticated than that. The film follows Dani, played by Florence Pugh in a career-making performance, as she joins her emotionally absent boyfriend on a trip to a Swedish midsummer festival that turns out to be run by a pagan death cult. By the end of the film, Dani has smiled. The cult has given her something her relationship could not: a sense of belonging. Aster's argument, delivered through some of the most beautiful horror cinematography ever shot, is that traditional relationships fail women, that emotional neglect is a form of violence, and that a community of women who burn men alive is a step up from a boyfriend who forgets your birthday. A trad score of 2.4 reflects the near-total absence of anything traditional audiences can hold onto.

#8: MaXXXine

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 31.5 TRAD: 13.6 MARGIN: -17.9 WOKE

Genre: Horror/Crime • Platform: Theatrical • Year: 2024

Ti West's X trilogy ends the way it began: with a woman who refuses to apologize for wanting more. MaXXXine drops Mia Goth's Maxine Minx into 1985 Los Angeles, where the Night Stalker prowls the streets, the Moral Majority screams from every television, and a sleazy private investigator tracks her every move. The film positions Maxine's pornographic ambitions as a heroic act of self-actualization against the conservative culture warriors of the Reagan era. The Moral Majority is the real villain of the film, not the seral killer. West frames 1980s moral panic as the true horror, while the porn industry is presented as a site of female empowerment. The woke score of 31.5 is driven by the film's complete investment in the idea that sexual liberation is the highest good and that anyone who opposes it, particularly religious conservatives, is the enemy. The 1980s period dressing is immaculate. The argument underneath is pure 2024.

#9: Lisa Frankenstein

WOKE LEAN WOKE: 21.0 TRAD: 4.2 MARGIN: -16.8 WOKE

Genre: Horror • Platform: Theatrical (Focus Features) • Year: 2024

Lisa Frankenstein is Diablo Cody's Jennifer's Body with worse reviews and a better aesthetic. Cody has been writing the same film since 2007: a misfit teenage girl discovers her power through violence, usually directed at men, and the audience is expected to cheer. Here the misfit is Lisa, a goth outcast in 1989 who reanimates a Victorian corpse and falls in love with him. When people get in their way, they die. The film frames Lisa's murder spree as a charming journey of self-discovery. The men are disposable. The women who oppose her are villains. The 1980s setting allows the film to position Lisa's violence against a backdrop of Reagan-era conformity, which the film treats as the real monster. The trad score of 4.2 reflects a film that has nothing traditional to offer and no interest in offering it.

#10: Presence

BALANCED WOKE WOKE: 22.4 TRAD: 8.4 MARGIN: -14.0 WOKE

Genre: Horror/Thriller • Platform: Theatrical • Year: 2025

Presence is the most technically inventive horror film of early 2025. It is also a quietly progressive film wearing an arthouse horror mask. Steven Soderbergh's latest finds the director in full experiment mode: the entire film is shot from the ghost's point of view. We float through a house, watching a family unravel. The daughter is grieving a lost friend. The son is an entitled athlete. The mother is a workaholic. The father is checked out. As the ghost's identity and intentions are revealed, the film's ideological commitments come into focus: the family's dysfunction maps neatly onto progressive critiques of patriarchal family structures, with the son as the embodiment of toxic masculinity and the daughter as the deserving victim of male violence. Soderbergh is too skilled to lecture, but the film's sympathies are unambiguous. A woke score over 22 tells you the quiet parts are still there even when whispered.

#11: Get Out

BALANCED WOKE WOKE: 16.1 TRAD: 4.0 MARGIN: -12.1 WOKE

Genre: Horror / Psychological Thiller • Platform: Theatrical (Uniersal Pictures / Blumhouse) • Director: Jordan Peele • Year: 2017

Let's be direct about what Get Out is. It is a very effective horror film built on a very specific political argument: white liberals are the primary threat to Black Americans, they disguise their predation behind progressive language, and the appropriate response to their overtures of allyship is deep paranoia. Daniel Kaluya plays Chris, a Black photographer who visits his white girlfriend's family and discovers they are harvesting Black bodies for white consciousness. The Armitage family does not use slurs. They talk about how much they admire Black athletes. They vote Democrat. And they are monsters. Jordan Peele's debut is as tight as a drum, funny and frightening in equal measure, and it earned its Best Original Screenplay Oscar. It is also a film whose argument, that white liberalism is a more insidious form of racism than open bigotry, has shaped a decade of horror films that followed. A trad score of 4.0 confirms there is almost nothing here for a traditional audience to hold onto.

#12: The First Omen

BALANCED WOKE WOKE: 15.3 TRAD: 7.9 MARGIN: -7.4 WOKE

Genre: Horror / Supernatural Thriller • Platform: Theatrical • Director: Arkasha Stevenson • Year: 2024

The 1976 Omen is one of the great supernatural horror films. Robert Thorn is an American diplomat who discovers his son may be the Antichrist. The film is about a father trying to protect his family from a truth too terrible to accept. The Church figures as an ally, however imperfect. Arkasha Stevenson's 2024 prequel takes the same universe and flips the moral axis. Here the Church is not the flawed ally fighting the Antichrist. The Church is creating the Antichrist, through a conspiracy of male clergy who rape and impregnate women against their will. Nell Tiger Free plays Margaret, a young American novitiate who uncovers the plot. The film is well-shot and genuinely frightening. But it transforms The Omen from a story about resisting evil into a story about the institution of the Church as the primary evil. The trad score of 7.9 is higher than some entries on this list because the film does retain elements of spiritual warfare, but the ideological rewiring of the original is unmistakable.

#13: The Ugly Stepsister

BALANCED WOKE WOKE: 18.1 TRAD: 11.2 MARGIN: -6.9 WOKE

Genre: Satrical Black Comedy/Body Horror • Platform: Theatrical • Director: Emile Blichfeldt • Year: 2025

Emile Blichfeldt looked at Cinderella and asked the question nobody wanted answered: what would the ugly stepsister's story actually feel like if you told it honestly? The answer involves tapeworms, butcher knives, and more blood than the original fairy tale could contain. Lea Myren plays Elvira, the ugly stepsister, who submits to increasingly grotesque body modifications in a desperate attempt to compete with her beautiful stepsister for the prince's attention. The film is a body-horror satire of beauty standards that frames Elvira's self-destruction as society's fault rather than her own. The prince is a vapid idiot. The stepmother is a monster who manufactures female insecurity. Cinderella herself is revealed to be as trapped as Elvira. The film is shot beautifully and performed fearlessly, but its argument is standard-issue progressive feminism: beauty culture is patriarchal violence, and the women who participate in it are victims, not agents. The slightly higher trad score reflects the film's genuine if grotesque craft, not any ideological balance.

#14: Forbidden Fruits

BALANCED WOKE WOKE: 27.8 TRAD: 21.2 MARGIN: -6.6 WOKE

There is a store at the Highland Place mall called Free Eden. It sells cupcake-scented candles, charm bracelets, and the kind of overpriced cotton sundress that costs more than your rent. The employees are gorgeous. They have their own slang. They only hire women. And they are a cult. Forbidden Fruits is a shopping-mall horror film about the intersection of consumer capitalism and feminist spirituality that is exactly as messy as that description suggests. The film's woke score of 27.8 is driven by its presentation of corporate feminism as an authentic alternative to traditional religion, while its trad score of 21.2 reflects the fact that the "cult" is ultimately revealed to be as controlling and predatory as the patriarchal structures it claims to oppose. This is one of the more ideologically confused entries on the list, which is why the margin is modest. It cannot decide whether feminist spirituality is the solution or just another problem, so it tries to be both.

#16: The Crow

BALANCED WOKE WOKE: 13.9 TRAD: 8.4 MARGIN: -5.5 WOKE

Genre: Action / Horror / Romnce • Platform: Theatrical • Director: Ruper Sanders • Year: 2024

The original Crow is one of those films that should not work as well as it does. Brandon Lee plays a murdered rock musician who comes back from the dead to kill the people who murdered him and his fiancee, guided by a literal crow. It is gothic melodrama that somehow became a genuine cultural artifact, partly because of Lee's tragic death during production and partly because Alex Proyas shot it like a music video directed by Edgar Allan Poe. The 2024 remake, starring Bill Skarsgard and FKA Twigs, attempts to modernize the material by making Shelly Webster, the fiancee, a more active participant in her own story. Eric and Shelly are now co-victims of a conspiracy involving a demonic crime lord played by Danny Huston. The problem is that the "modernization" transforms the original's simple love-and-revenge story into a story about female agency and institutional corruption. The original's power came from its purity: love transcending death. The remake replaces that with progressive messaging that dilutes the myth.

#18: Us

Jordan Peele's Us has a problem that Get Out did not have: it is trying to do too many things. Get Out was a scalpel. Us is a Swiss Army knife that does not quite close back up properly. The setup is excellent. The Wilson family, headed by Adelaide (Lupita Nyongo), is terrorized at their beach house by doppelgangers in red jumpsuits wielding golden scissors. The Tethered, as they are called, are the abandoned underclass who have been living in tunnels beneath America, mimicking the surface world's every movement. The film falls on the woke side of the ledger because its political argument, even if muddled in the details, is clear in its sympathies. The Tethered are victims. Society created them. #20: Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness

BALANCED WOKE: 13.6 TRAD: 10.2 MARGIN: -3.4 WOKE

Genre: Action/Adventure/Horror • Platform: Theatrical • Year: 2022

Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness is the MCU's most interesting mess. Sam Raimi brings genuine horror filmmaking to a franchise that desperately needed it, Elizabeth Olsen delivers a villain performance for the ages, and the multiverse concept allows for genuinely creative visual sequences. The woke score reflects the film's handling of Wanda Maximoff, who is transformed from a grieving mother into a monster whose grief is used to justify atrocity, only to be redeemed by a last-minute realization that she has become the villain. The Pattern Parents Should Notice

Twenty horror films. Twenty progressive arguments. The pattern is not subtle once you look for it.

The three most frequent targets: Christianity (Heretic, Immaculate, Leviticus, The First Omen), traditional gender roles and family structures (The Bride!, The Substance, Midsommar, MaXXXine, Lisa Frankenstein), and capitalism (Clown in a Cornfield, Death of a Uniicorn, Ready or Not 2). The one target that never appears: progressive ideology itself. No horror film in the VirtueVigil database is about a woke cult that consumes its members. No slasher features a final girl who discovers that her progressive community is the real threat. The ideological blind spot is the blind spot.

This matters because horror is a gateway genre. Teenagers and young adults consume horror in enormous quantities. It shapes their sense of what is scary, what is threatening, and what is worth fighting. When every monster looks like Christianity, tradition, or capitalism, and never like progressivism, the genre is not just entertaining. It is training an audience.

The good news, reflected in the diminishing margins as this list descends from #1 to #20, is that most woke horror films overplay their hand. A score margin of minus 27 (Heretic) is an ideological howl. A margin of minus 3 (Doctor Strange) is background radiation. The films that lecture hardest are the films that date fastest. The films that trust their audience, that let horror be horror without the footnotes, are the films that endure. Parents who want to know what their teenagers are absorbing should start at the top of this list and work down.
