Science fiction has always been the genre that asks the biggest questions. What does it mean to be human? What are we willing to sacrifice to survive? When does progress become hubris? For decades, the best science fiction films explored these questions without a political agenda. They trusted the audience to think. They did not demand that the audience conform.

That has changed. Modern sci-fi is increasingly a delivery vehicle for progressive ideology: climate panic, anti-corporate messaging, gender deconstruction, and the steady erosion of traditional family structures. The films that once imagined the future with wonder and moral seriousness now spend their runtime making sure you know which side of the culture war they are on.

This list is different. These are the 15 most traditional sci-fi films in the VirtueVigil database, ranked by their traditional margin score. The margin is the gap between a film's traditional score and its woke score. The higher the gap, the more the film honors faith, family, sacrifice, duty, and moral clarity without hedging or apologizing. Every film on this list was made by people who either believed in something worth defending or trusted their audience enough not to lecture them. That matters.

Every entry links to the full VirtueVigil review with complete trope breakdowns, scoring methodology, and content warnings. If you are looking for sci-fi you can watch with your family without the ideological baggage, start here.

  1. 1

    Project Hail Mary (2026)

    STRONGLY TRADITIONAL +20.8 TRAD

    Project Hail Mary opens with a man waking up alone on a spacecraft with no memory of who he is or why he is there. It ends with him choosing to stay, alone, in a distant solar system, because someone he met along the way needed him. That is the arc. A man who discovers his purpose is not glory or survival but self-sacrificial service to another. The film, adapted from Andy Weir's novel and directed by Phil Lord and Christopher Miller, scores a +20.8 TRAD margin by doing what the best sci-fi always did: using the extraordinary to illuminate the ordinary truths about courage, friendship, and the moral obligation to help those who cannot help themselves. There is no ideological subtext here. There is only a man in a small ship deciding that his life is worth spending for the right reason.

    Star Wars: Episode IV - A New Hope (1977)

    STRONGLY TRADITIONAL +20.8 TRAD

    Star Wars did not arrive quietly. On May 25, 1977, George Lucas released a film that redefined what movies could be, and nearly fifty years on, it still holds up as one of the most purely traditional pieces of popular entertainment ever made. A farm boy hears a call to adventure, finds a mentor who teaches him about a higher power, rescues a princess, and destroys the ultimate weapon of an evil empire. Every beat is drawn from the Hero's Journey that Joseph Campbell mapped out, and Lucas never apologizes for it. The Force is not a metaphor. It is a moral order: light side versus dark side, good versus evil, selflessness versus domination. With a woke score of just 3.4 and a traditional score of 24.1, A New Hope earns its +20.8 margin by being exactly what it set out to be: a myth for the modern age, told with conviction and without irony.

    A Quiet Place (2018)

    STRONGLY TRADITIONAL +19.6 TRAD

    A Quiet Place is, without question, one of the most pro-family mainstream studio films of the past decade. The premise is simple: alien creatures hunt by sound, and if you make a noise, you die. The Abbott family has survived by learning to live in silence, communicating through sign language, and protecting each other at all costs. But the horror is not the point. The film is about what parents are willing to do for their children. Lee Abbott, played by John Krasinski, spends the entire runtime demonstrating that fatherhood is not a soft role. It is a role that requires sacrifice, preparation, and the willingness to lay down your life. The closing shot of Emily Blunt cocking a shotgun with her daughter at her side is not a feminist statement. It is a mother protecting her family. The traditional score of 22.5 against a woke score of 2.9 reflects a film that understands what families actually value.

    Godzilla Minus One (2023)

    TRADITIONAL +19.2 TRAD

    Godzilla Minus One is the best Godzilla film made in decades, and also one of the most traditionally coded action films of recent memory. The story centers on Koichi Shikishima, a kamikaze pilot who failed to die for his country and now carries the shame of that failure into postwar Japan. When Godzilla emerges from the irradiated Pacific, Shikishima is given a second chance: not to die, but to fight. The film treats duty, honor, and personal redemption as real things. There is no ironic distance. Shikishima's arc is not about deconstructing patriotism. It is about a man who has to learn that living for something is harder than dying for nothing. With a strong traditional score of 24.8 and a woke score of just 5.6, this film earns its place among the best traditional sci-fi ever made.

    Star Wars: Episode V - The Empire Strikes Back (1980)

    TRADITIONAL +18.6 TRAD

    The Empire Strikes Back is the rare sequel that improves on everything the original established. It does this not by escalating spectacle but by deepening everything else: character, theme, moral complexity. Luke Skywalker's training with Yoda on Dagobah is one of the most spiritually serious sequences in blockbuster filmmaking. The lesson is not about power. It is about mastery of self. The dark side cave is not a monster. It is a mirror. Luke fails because he brings his weapons inside, and the film treats that failure as a moral education, not a plot point. The traditional score of 20.3 against a woke score of 1.7 makes this one of the cleanest scores in the entire sci-fi genre. Decades before Hollywood started injecting political messaging into every frame, Irvin Kershner made a sequel about patience, faith, and the cost of impatience. It has aged better than almost anything else of its era.

    War Machine (2026)

    TRADITIONAL +18.0 TRAD

    War Machine is a glorious throwback to the testosterone-fueled sci-fi action films of the 1980s and 1990s, and it does not apologize for it. Patrick Hughes has made a film that could sit comfortably on a shelf next to Predator and Commando without anyone blinking. A squad of elite soldiers faces an alien threat, and the film treats their competence, their loyalty to each other, and their willingness to fight as virtues, not pathologies. With a traditional score of 22 and a woke score of just 4, War Machine is not a film that hedges. It knows exactly what it is: a military sci-fi action picture where the good guys are good guys, the mission is righteous, and no one stops in the third act to question whether they deserve to win. That clarity is what earns it the +18 margin and a spot on this list.

    Logan (2017)

    TRADITIONAL +17.7 TRAD

    There is a scene near the end of Logan where Laura cradles the dying Wolverine, turns the cross marking his grave sideways to make an X, and cries for the first time in the film. If that moment does not move you, check your pulse. Logan is the rare superhero film that understands fatherhood as a sacred burden, not a demographic opportunity. James Mangold and Hugh Jackman made a western about a broken man who discovers, through the daughter he never wanted, that his life has meaning after all. The traditional score of 23.5 with a woke score of 5.8 yields a +17.7 margin, and every point of that was earned. Laura is not a girl-power symbol. She is a child who needs a father, and Logan is the man who learns, far too late, that being a father is the only thing he was ever good at.

    Inception (2010)

    TRADITIONAL +17.5 TRAD

    Christopher Nolan spent ten years writing Inception. It shows. This is one of the most structurally ambitious mainstream films ever produced: a heist story operating across five simultaneous dream levels, each with its own physics and its own stakes. But the architecture is not what makes Inception traditional. What makes it traditional is that the entire film rests on one question: can Dom Cobb get home to his children? Everything else -- the corporate espionage, the dream technology, the zero-gravity hallway fights -- is in service of a father trying to return to his family. Cobb's guilt over his wife's death is real. His love for his children is real. And the film's famous final shot does not ask you to question reality. It asks you to understand what matters more than reality. A traditional score of 20.4 against a woke score of 2.9 makes this one of the most ideologically clean blockbusters of the past two decades.

    Back to the Future (1985)

    TRADITIONAL +17.2 TRAD

    Back to the Future is the most quietly conservative film of the 1980s, and Robert Zemeckis and Bob Gale probably never thought about it that way. The premise sounds like science fiction but operates as a moral argument: a teenager travels back to 1955 and discovers that his parents' generation was better than his own. The 1950s town square is cleaner, safer, and more dignified than the 1985 strip mall version Marty McFly left behind. The film's central question is whether Marty can repair his family by repairing the past, and the answer is an unqualified yes. Strong fathers, loving mothers, and communities where people know each other are presented not as nostalgia but as the foundation of a good life. At a traditional 18.2 with a woke score of just 1.0, Back to the Future scores +17.2 TRAD, and the interesting thing is that it was never trying to score anything. It was just telling the truth as the filmmakers saw it.

    Predator (1987)

    TRADITIONAL +17.1 TRAD

    Predator is 107 minutes of genre filmmaking executed at an extremely high level. It is also, quietly, a film that has gotten more interesting over the decades as the culture around it has shifted. A team of elite commandos, played by some of the most physically imposing actors of the era, is hunted by an alien trophy-seeker in the jungle. There is no subtext about toxic masculinity. There is no third-act speech about systemic violence. Dutch and his men are warriors fighting an enemy they do not understand, and when they realize their weapons are useless, they adapt. Dutch's final confrontation with the Predator strips away every advantage: no guns, no team, no technology. Just a man, mud, fire, and a trap. It is one of the most effective celebrations of masculine resourcefulness ever put on film, and its traditional score of 18.5 with a woke score of 1.4 places it squarely on this list.

    The Fantastic Four: First Steps (2026)

    TRADITIONAL +15.9 TRAD

    The Fantastic Four has been one of the most difficult Marvel properties to adapt to film for twenty years. Two Fox attempts that ranged from disappointing to catastrophic left a generation of comics readers convinced the First Family could never work on screen. First Steps proves them wrong. The film understands something the previous versions did not: the Fantastic Four are not superheroes in the conventional sense. They are a family. Reed Richards is a husband and father who happens to be the smartest man alive. Sue Storm is a wife and mother who happens to be invisible and capable of generating force fields. Their dynamic is not will-they-won't-they. It is we-already-did-now-we-protect-what-we-built. The traditional score of 19 with a woke score of 3.1 makes this the most ideologically clean MCU entry in years.

    Guardians of the Galaxy (2014)

    TRADITIONAL +15.5 TRAD

    Guardians of the Galaxy is one of the most purely enjoyable films the MCU ever produced, and by our scoring system it is also one of the most traditionally coded. A group of criminals, misfits, and orphans discover that they are capable of something none of them expected: sacrificial love for each other. Peter Quill's arc is not about processing childhood trauma. It is about finally opening the last gift his dying mother gave him. Groot's sacrifice is a genuine act of self-giving. The film earns a +15.5 TRAD margin by understanding that the found-family trope works best when the family actually forms -- not when it deconstructs. Traditional values are not mocked here. They are the prize the characters spend the whole film earning.

    Interstellar (2014)

    TRADITIONAL +15.4 TRAD

    Interstellar is Christopher Nolan's most emotionally direct film. Where Inception hides its heart inside puzzle boxes, Interstellar puts its heart on the table in the first ten minutes and then spends the next three hours testing whether it will break. Cooper leaves his daughter Murph behind to save humanity, and the film never lets him, or the audience, forget what that costs. The scene where Cooper watches twenty-three years of missed messages in a few minutes is one of the most devastating sequences in modern cinema. It is not about the science. It is about a father realizing he cannot get the time back. The traditional score of 23.2 against a woke score of 7.7 reflects that Nolan's commitment to the father-daughter bond is absolute, and the film's resolution, love as a force that transcends dimensions, is more theological than it is scientific.

    Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015)

    TRADITIONAL +14.5 TRAD

    Age of Ultron is often treated as the forgotten middle child of the Avengers films, but by VirtueVigil scoring it earns a strong +14.5 TRAD margin that pulls ahead of many MCU entries. The reason is the Hawkeye farm. Clint Barton, the Avenger everyone underestimates, has a pregnant wife named Laura, two kids named Cooper and Lila, and a farmhouse with a porch. When the team is broken, Clint brings them home. The film treats that home as sacred ground. The family is not a weakness the villain exploits. It is the thing the heroes are fighting for. Joss Whedon, for all his later controversies, understood that the Avengers needed a reason to fight beyond saving the world. A traditional score of 18.3 with a woke score of just 3.9 makes this one of the most family-affirming superhero films in the canon.

    Backrooms (2026)

    TRADITIONAL +14.4 TRAD

    Here is what Backrooms is: a door opens in the basement of a furniture showroom. That door should not be there. People who go through it find themselves in a space that is geometrically impossible, functionally infinite, and quietly hostile. There are no jump scares. There is no villain monologue. There is only the creeping dread of being somewhere that was not built for human beings and does not care whether you make it out. The film earns its +14.4 TRAD margin the old-fashioned way: by treating survival as a moral exercise rather than a political metaphor. The characters do not represent identity groups. They are just people, lost and scared, and the film respects them enough to let their choices carry the weight. With a traditional score of 19.3 and a woke score of just 4.9, Backrooms is proof that thoughtful sci-fi horror can still exist without the ideological freight.

Why This List Matters

Fifteen films. The oldest is from 1977. The newest is from this year. What unites them is not era or budget or studio. It is values. Every film on this list trusts its audience to think without telling them what to think. Every one treats family, sacrifice, courage, and moral clarity as goods, not problems to be deconstructed. The woke score on these films averages just 3.9. The traditional score averages 20.9. That gap tells you everything you need to know about the state of the genre.

Sci-fi is not supposed to be a lecture hall. It is supposed to be a place where big ideas can be explored with intellectual honesty. The films on this list do that. If you are tired of sitting down to watch a movie and discovering thirty minutes in that you are being scolded, start here. These are the films that respect you.
