Netflix is the largest content factory on earth. It spent approximately $17 billion on original programming in 2025 alone, producing everything from prestige dramas to algorithm-targeted action vehicles to animated features no one asked for. That scale makes it the single most important laboratory for understanding where entertainment ideology is heading. This ranking applies VirtueVigil's full dual-scoring methodology to every major Netflix Original in our database, sorted from wokest to most traditional.

The results are not what the platform's brand positioning would predict. Netflix markets itself as progressive and inclusive, and its award-season slate (Emilia Perez, Glass Onion) reflects that branding unambiguously. But the platform's actual output is far more ideologically diverse than the discourse suggests. Action films, legacy sequels, and unapologetic comedies populate the traditional end of the ranking in numbers that complicate the narrative of Netflix as a uniformly progressive institution. The data reveals a platform that knows exactly what different audience segments want and serves all of them without loyalty to any single ideological position.

Rankings run from #1 (highest woke score relative to traditional) to #20 (strongest traditional margin). Every entry links to its full VirtueVigil review with trope-by-trope breakdown, parental guidance, and complete scoring methodology. The data built this list. Netflix built the films.

#4: Adolescence (2025)

WOKE LEAN TRAD: 14.8 WOKE: 22.5 MARGIN: 7.8 WOKE

Director: Philip Barantini • Stars: Stephen Graham, Owen Cooper, Christine Tremarco • Year: 2025

Adolescence is the most important piece of television produced in 2025, and that is not the same thing as saying it is the best. The premise is devastatingly simple: armed police raid a family home in South Yorkshire at dawn and drag 13-year-old Jamie Miller out of bed on suspicion of murder. The series is shot in four continuous takes, each an hour-long episode following a different perspective on the crime and its aftermath. The craft is undeniable. The ideological framework is equally undeniable: the series builds toward an argument about toxic online masculinity radicalizing young boys, and it does not entertain counterarguments. The traditional score reflects the procedural rigor, the family's grief, and the raw power of Stephen Graham's performance as Jamie's father. The woke score captures the monomaniacal focus on male violence as a systemic rather than individual phenomenon. It is a series that wants to be a policy document as much as a drama.

#8: All Quiet on the Western Front (2022)

WOKE LEAN TRAD: 9.0 WOKE: 13.9 MARGIN: 4.9 WOKE

Director: Edward Berger • Stars: Felix Kammerer, Albrecht Schuch, Aaron Hilmer • Year: 2022

Edward Berger's German adaptation of Remarque's 1929 novel won four Oscars including Best International Feature, and the craft achievements are undeniable. The cinematography, sound design, and battle sequences are among the most visceral ever committed to film. The ideological agenda is equally undeniable. Berger made a film that is more anti-war and specifically anti-German-military than Remarque's novel ever was, adding an armistice-negotiation subplot that frames the German high command as venal war criminals who sent boys to die for ego. The anti-war position is not inherently progressive, but Berger's framing eliminates any distinction between justified and unjustified military action, the distinction that separates traditional anti-war films (Paths of Glory) from progressive ones (All Quiet 2022). The result is technically brilliant and ideologically engineered, a film that uses its craft to advance an argument it never acknowledges making.

Hit Man is the most pleasant surprise of 2024, and its MIXED verdict with an even score split is the most honest assessment of what the film is: ideologically disinterested. #17: Extraction (2020)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 16.5 WOKE: 4.8 MARGIN: 11.7 TRAD

Director: Sam Hargrave • Stars: Chris Hemsworth, Rudhraksh Jaiswal, Randeep Hooda • Year: 2020

Extraction is a very good action film that got dismissed by critics and embraced by audiences, which is probably the more accurate verdict. Chris Hemsworth plays Tyler Rake, a mercenary hired to extract the kidnapped son of an Indian drug lord from Dhaka, Bangladesh. The job goes wrong immediately and never stops going wrong. The film is essentially one continuous deterioration of a rescue mission into a survival gauntlet, and Sam Hargrave, a former stunt coordinator, shoots action geography with a clarity most directors never achieve. The traditional score reflects everything the film values: a man's competence under pressure, the moral obligation to protect a child even when the job goes sideways, and the redemption arc of a broken soldier finding something worth dying for. The woke score is low because the film has no ideological agenda beyond the rescue. Extraction does not critique the drug trade, does not comment on Western intervention in South Asia, does not interrogate Tyler Rake's psychology. It just shows you a man doing his job while everything burns around him. That purity of purpose is exactly what the TRADITIONAL verdict rewards.

Extraction 2 does something rare in action sequels: it actually justifies its existence. The first Extraction ended with Tyler Rake apparently dying in Dhaka, but the sequel opens with his recovery and rehabilitation, pulling him back into action to rescue the family of his ex-wife's sister from a Georgian prison. The film's centerpiece is a 21-minute continuous shot that moves from prison riot to armored train assault to helicopter chase, and it is one of the most technically accomplished action sequences ever filmed. The traditional score is slightly higher than the first film because Extraction 2 leans harder into the family-rescue premise: Tyler Rake is not saving a stranger's child this time but his own estranged family, however tangentially connected. The woke score is even lower because the film strips away every remaining progressive trapping. There is no critique of mercenary culture, no hand-wringing about violence, and no diversity-by-quota casting. Golshifteh Farahani and Adam Bessa are genuine partners because the story demands them, not because a spreadsheet required representation. This is action cinema stripped to its essential elements, and the scoring reflects that purity.

War Machine is a glorious throwback to the testosterone-fueled sci-fi action films of the 1980s and 1990s, and it does not apologize for it. Netflix spent real money on a film that would have been at home in a 1987 multiplex, and the VirtueVigil scoring system recognizes a kindred spirit: entertainment that knows what it is and refuses to be anything else.

The Netflix Originals catalog, examined through VirtueVigil's dual-scoring methodology, reveals a platform far more ideologically diverse than either its detractors or champions acknowledge. The top of this ranking is dominated by award-bait progressivism (Glass Onion, Emilia Perez) and YA-adjacent genre programming (Enola Holmes 3, Wednesday) that wears its politics on its sleeve. The bottom is populated by action films (Extraction, War Machine) and legacy comedy (Happy Gilmore 2) that treat ideological signaling as an irrelevance. The middle is where Netflix's real character lives: films like Zero Day, Hit Man, and The Old Guard that contain competing ideological impulses and refuse to resolve them.

The implication is not that Netflix is secretly conservative. It is that the platform's business model, which requires serving hundreds of millions of subscribers across every demographic and political orientation, produces content that cannot be reduced to a single ideological profile. Netflix makes what audiences watch. Audiences are complicated. The ranking reflects that complexity without trying to resolve it.
