Faith-based cinema is one of the most underserved and misunderstood categories in American entertainment. For decades, Hollywood ignored the audience entirely. When it did pay attention, the results were often condescending, hostile, or incompetent. The rise of Angel Studios, the Kendrick brothers, and independent faith filmmakers has changed the economics, but the critical establishment still does not know what to do with movies that take God seriously.

This list ranks every faith-based and religiously-themed film and show in our database of 815+ reviews, from most traditional to most woke. Some entries are explicit Christian productions (Fireproof, The Passion of the Christ, David). Others are mainstream films with strong religious themes or Catholic imagery (The Exorcist, Immaculate, The Nun II). A few use faith language ironically or antagonistically (Is God Is). We include them all so the picture is complete.

The ranking is by VirtueVigil margin: tradScore minus wokeScore. Positive margin means traditional values dominate. Negative means woke content dominates. A few films on this list are among the most traditional we have ever scored. One is among the most woke. The spread tells its own story about how faith is treated across the industry: with reverence at one end, with fury at the other, and with almost nothing in between. Ranked from most traditional to most woke.

#1 (Most Traditional): Fireproof (2008)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 88.0 | MARGIN: +88 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Christian Drama / Romance • Year: 2008

Fireproof sits alone at the top of our entire database with a staggering +88 traditional margin, the highest of any film we have ever reviewed. The Kendrick brothers' film about a firefighter fighting to save his marriage through a 40-day "Love Dare" challenge is functionally a sermon with characters. It knows exactly what it is and makes no apologies. Kirk Cameron's Caleb Holt transforms from a pornography-consuming, divorce-bound husband into a man who learns that love is not a feeling but a daily act of sacrificial will. The Christian messaging is neither subtext nor subtlety --- it is the entire text. At +88, Fireproof is not trying to be cinema. It is trying to save marriages, and on its own terms it succeeds.

#2: It's a Wonderful Life (1946)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 55.9 | MARGIN: +55 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Drama, Fantasy, Christmas • Year: 1946

The most traditional American film ever made, and it accomplishes this without ever feeling like propaganda. Frank Capra's 1946 masterpiece about George Bailey, a man who spends his entire life sacrificing his dreams for the people of Bedford Falls, was a commercial failure on release and has since become the definitive Christmas film in American culture. The argument is devastating in its simplicity: a life spent serving others is not a failure, and the measure of a man is not his bank account but the hole he leaves in his community when he is gone. The faith is woven in, not pasted on --- George's desperate prayer in the bar, Clarence the angel, the divine intervention that is never named as such but is unmistakably there. At +55, It's a Wonderful Life is the gold standard for how faith themes can produce art that outlasts every trend.

#3: The Passion of the Christ (2004)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 45.3 | MARGIN: +45 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Biblical Drama • Year: 2004

Mel Gibson did not make a movie. He made an act of witness. The Passion of the Christ begins in Gethsemane, in the dark, with Jesus already in agony, and proceeds through the most unflinching depiction of the crucifixion ever filmed. There is no backstory, no context for the uninitiated. Gibson assumes you know the story and refuses to hold your hand. The film grossed $612 million worldwide against a $30 million budget, proved that faith audiences were a massively underserved market, and was met with exactly the kind of institutional hostility you would expect from an industry that found its conviction threatening. The zero woke score reflects a film that simply does not acknowledge progressive ideology exists. At +45, it remains the highest-grossing R-rated film in American history.

#6: American Martyr: The Stanley Rother Story (2026)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 24.6 | MARGIN: +23 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Documentary, Faith • Year: 2026

Stanley Francis Rother was an Oklahoma farm boy who barely passed Latin in seminary and was told he might never be ordained. He became a priest anyway. Assigned to a remote Guatemalan village during a civil war that targeted clergy, Rother refused to leave when the death squads came. He was murdered in his rectory in 1981. American Martyr tells his story with the documentary rigor it deserves: no sensationalism, no agenda beyond the facts, no attempt to make Rother into a culture-war symbol. He was simply a man who believed his vows meant something when staying meant dying. At +23, it is one of the most quietly radical faith films we have ever reviewed --- radical in its refusal to add anything to the story beyond the story itself.

#7: The Chosen: Last Supper (2025)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 23.8 | MARGIN: +23 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Biblical Drama / Religious Epic • Year: 2025

There are moments in The Chosen: Last Supper that feel genuinely sacred. That is a word rarely applied to film. When Jonathan Roumie's Jesus lowers himself to wash the feet of his disciples, and you watch the confusion and shame and love on their faces, the scene lands with a weight that most religious cinema never achieves. The Chosen series has always been an anomaly: a crowd-funded biblical drama that became a global phenomenon without a single studio executive. Last Supper takes the series into its most consequential territory, the hours before the arrest, and treats the material with the solemnity of a liturgical act. The 0.85 woke score is negligible --- a whisper of modern sensibility in an otherwise thoroughly traditional telling.

#8: Young Washington (2026)

Young Washington is the most confident film Angel Studios has ever released. Jon Erwin, the director who built a faith-based empire on modest budgets and underserved audiences, has taken on his most ambitious subject: the early life of George Washington, before the uniform, before the presidency, when he was a young surveyor and soldier whose character was forged in the wilderness. #9: I Can Only Imagine (2018)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 22.0 | MARGIN: +22 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Drama, Faith, Music • Year: 2018

I Can Only Imagine is one of the most straightforwardly traditional Christian films ever to receive wide theatrical distribution, and it earned every dollar of its $86 million box office haul honestly. The film tells the story of Bart Millard, the lead singer of MercyMe, and the writing of the song "I Can Only Imagine" --- the best-selling Christian single of all time. But the film is not really about the song. It is about Millard's relationship with his abusive father, a monster who beat him and his mother, and the almost incomprehensible act of forgiveness that redeemed both of them. Dennis Quaid plays the father as a man so consumed by rage that his eventual conversion feels miraculous precisely because the film has not softened what came before. At +22, it is the rare faith film that works as both testimony and cinema.

#10: Father Stu (2022)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 21.8 | MARGIN: +20 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Biography / Drama • Year: 2022

Mark Wahlberg paid for this film himself. Sony looked at the premise --- a Catholic boxing biopic about a man who embraces progressive muscle disease as participation in Christ's suffering --- and declined to fund it. Wahlberg believed in it enough to write the check. Father Stu earned $21.8 million on a $4 million budget, scored a 97% audience rating on Rotten Tomatoes against a 43% critic score, and became one of the most discussed faith films of the decade. The 54-point gap between critics and audiences tells the story: this is a movie made for the faithful that critics do not have the framework to evaluate. Stuart Long's transformation from amateur boxer to priest, his acceptance of a disease that slowly paralyzed and killed him, and his insistence that suffering has meaning is genuinely radical in a culture that treats suffering as an engineering problem to be solved. At +20, Father Stu is as Catholic as movies get.

#12: Jesus Revolution (2023)

The critic consensus on Jesus Revolution is 54 percent on Rotten Tomatoes. The audience score is 99 percent. That gap tells you everything you need to know about how professional film critics and ordinary Americans evaluate a film about the Jesus Movement of the 1970s. Kelsey Grammer plays Chuck Smith, the staid pastor of a dying California church who reluctantly opens his doors to a wave of hippie converts led by Lonnie Frisbee (Jonathan Roumie). The film tells a true story --- the Calvary Chapel movement that baptized thousands of counterculture kids --- without irony, without apology, and without the protective distance that secular filmmakers always insert when handling religious revival. #13: Cabrini (2024)

TRADITIONAL TRAD: 14.8 | MARGIN: +12 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Biographical Drama / Faith / History • Year: 2024

Cabrini tells a story that mainstream Hollywood would never make, featuring a protagonist that contemporary progressive culture cannot quite process: a 19th-century Catholic nun who built hospitals, orphanages, and a religious empire across two continents through sheer force of will and an unshakeable belief that God wanted her to succeed. Francesca Cabrini arrived in New York in 1889 with five sisters and no money. She died in 1917 having founded 67 institutions. The film, from Angel Studios and the same team behind Sound of Freedom, is handsomely mounted and entirely sincere. The 2.7 woke score reflects a film that acknowledges the discrimination Cabrini faced as an Italian immigrant and a woman without ever making that the point. The point is what she built. At +12, Cabrini is faith cinema that competes on craft, not just conviction.

#14: The Exorcist (1973)

STRONGLY TRADITIONAL TRAD: 26.7 | MARGIN: +24 TRADITIONAL

Genre: Horror • Year: 1973

Wait --- a horror film on a faith-based list? The Exorcist is not merely a horror film. It is the most theologically serious mainstream film ever made about the reality of evil. William Friedkin and William Peter Blatty treated demonic possession not as metaphor or camp but as a literal confrontation between a priest and a supernatural enemy. Father Karras begins the film as a priest who has lost his faith. By the end, he has performed an exorcism that costs him his life, and he dies a believer. The film's Catholic framework is not set dressing --- it is the entire engine of the story. The 2.8 woke score is essentially zero for a 1973 film. At +24, The Exorcist is more traditionally religious than most films that market themselves as Christian.

#18: Is God Is (2026)

WOKE WOKE: 17.6 | MARGIN: -10 WOKE

Genre: Thriller / Drama / Revenge • Year: 2026

The title is the first provocation. Is God Is arrives as one of the most explicitly ideological films of 2026. Director Aleshea Harris has been clear about what she is making: a Black feminist revenge myth, a film about women's violent agency against a patriarch who destroyed their family. The stylized character names --- the Monster, Ruby the God, the Rough One --- signal from the first frame that this is not realism. This is closer to Greek tragedy adapted for the American South. Twin sisters Racine and Anaia, scarred by a father who murdered their mother, embark on a journey of revenge. The religious language in the title is deployed ironically --- the "God" here is not a redeemer but an absence. At -10, Is God Is is the first woke entry on this list and the only one that uses religious language as a deliberate provocation.

#19 (Most Woke): Immaculate (2024)

STRONGLY WOKE WOKE: 25.4 | MARGIN: -22 WOKE

Genre: Horror • Year: 2024

And then there is Immaculate. Sydney Sweeney spent years developing this film as a vehicle that would let her operate in the horror genre while carrying a message she clearly believed in. The result --- shot in Italy, dressed in Catholic iconography, structured as Rosemary's Baby filtered through contemporary reproductive politics --- became one of 2024's most discussed horror films and one of its most precisely crafted woke traps. A young American nun discovers she is pregnant despite being a virgin. The Church sees a miracle. The film sees a prison. Every Catholic symbol --- the convent, the habit, the crucifix, the pregnancy --- is inverted into a tool of patriarchal control. The 25.4 woke score is the highest on this list by a wide margin. Where The Exorcist treated Catholic theology as a weapon against evil, Immaculate treats it as the evil itself. At -22, it is the most woke faith-themed film we have ever reviewed and a perfect bookend to Fireproof's +88.
